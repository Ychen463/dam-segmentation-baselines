\begin{abstract}
Automated semantic segmentation of cracks and spalling on concrete dam surfaces is essential for structural health monitoring, yet existing methods optimise pixel-level objectives without preserving the structural continuity of detected defects.
We propose \textbf{TopoDistill}, a topology-aware segmentation framework that augments a SegFormer-B2 backbone with two complementary enhancements:
(1)~a \emph{Dynamic Snake Convolution} (DSConv) branch that captures elongated crack geometry through deformable sampling, adding only 4M parameters; and
(2)~\emph{Dual-Teacher Knowledge Distillation} (DTKD) that transfers complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher via per-class weighted ensemble, at zero additional inference cost. Agreement-aware pixel-level modulation of the distillation signal is investigated as an exploratory extension.
On the \textbf{DamSegment} dataset \citep{damsegment2026} (1{,}500 images, 3~classes), the primary method (DSConv+DTKD, row~c in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) achieves 72.3\% foreground mIoU---the highest among all compared methods---with DSConv driving crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ +2.8) and DTKD contributing +1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ on the original split, though this incremental gain exhibits split dependence ($-$0.6 on a feature-cluster split; Table~\ref{tab:group_split}). The full pipeline reduces cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3$\times$ (std 0.52 vs.\ 1.47, 3 seeds), with DSConv accounting for most of the reduction (std 0.70).
An investigation of Skeleton Recall Loss reveals an asymmetric effect in distillation: SRL benefits teachers but harms students, indicating that optimal training recipes differ across pipeline stages.
Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation shows that DSConv is the primary driver of cross-domain robustness, accounting for ${\sim}$80\% of the mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain (0.1\%$\to$14.9\%), with DTKD and SRL contributing a further +3.8.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Concrete dams are safety-critical infrastructure whose structural integrity must be continuously monitored throughout multi-decade service lives.
Surface defects---predominantly cracks and spalling---serve as early indicators of internal deterioration, yet their manual inspection remains labour-intensive, subjective, and logistically constrained by the scale and accessibility of dam faces \citep{chen2024damsurvey,li2024dam}.
Automated semantic segmentation of these defects from high-resolution imagery therefore promises substantial gains in inspection efficiency and repeatability.

Recent deep-learning methods have advanced pixel-level defect segmentation considerably.
Encoder--decoder architectures such as U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} and hierarchical transformers like SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} achieve competitive Intersection-over-Union (IoU) on benchmark crack datasets \citep{zou2019deepcrack,liu2019deepcrack}.
However, IoU and related region-overlap metrics are insensitive to topological errors: a prediction that fragments a continuous crack into disconnected segments may still score favourably on IoU while misrepresenting the structural extent of the defect \citep{pantoja2024cracktopology}.
For structural safety assessment, the \emph{connectivity} of a predicted crack is as important as its pixel coverage---a fragmented prediction can lead an engineer to underestimate propagation length and thus misjudge residual capacity.

Topology-preserving losses such as clDice \citep{shit2021cldice} and persistent-homology penalties \citep{hu2019topology,clough2020topological} partially address this gap, yet they either incur prohibitive GPU memory for high-resolution dam images or operate on binary masks without multi-class support.
The recently proposed Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} overcomes both limitations by computing recall on CPU-extracted ground-truth skeletons with over 90\% reduction in computational overhead.
Concurrently, Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} reshapes kernel sampling grids to follow elongated, tortuous paths---a morphological prior that aligns naturally with the geometry of cracks.

Meanwhile, large vision foundation models such as SAM \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} offer powerful pretrained representations, yet our experiments show that parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} on small industrial datasets underperforms fully fine-tuned task-specific architectures.
A natural question arises: \emph{can the complementary strengths of task-specific and foundation models be combined}?
We address this through knowledge distillation, where a student model learns from both a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher.

In this paper we propose \textbf{TopoDistill}, a framework that augments SegFormer-B2 with a parallel DSConv branch and dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD). We additionally investigate Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) and uncover an asymmetric role in distillation: SRL enriches teacher training but harms the student when combined with DTKD.
We evaluate on the semantic segmentation subset of the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, comprising 1{,}500 annotated dam surface images.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A topology-aware segmentation framework with two complementary enhancements: a parallel Dynamic Snake Convolution branch that drives crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ +2.8) through geometry-aware sampling, and dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD) that transfers complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher. On the original split, DTKD adds +1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ over DSConv alone; however, this incremental gain does not replicate on a feature-cluster split ($-$0.6), indicating split dependence. The full pipeline reduces cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3$\times$ vs.\ the baseline (std 0.52 vs.\ 1.47), though DSConv alone accounts for most of this reduction (std 0.70). SRL and DTKD jointly contribute +3.8 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ on S2DS cross-dataset transfer (SRL +2.2, DTKD +1.6). The primary method (DSConv+DTKD) adds only 4M parameters with no inference-time overhead from distillation. The main method uses manually chosen per-class teacher weights, though ablation shows equal weighting performs comparably (72.2 vs.\ 72.3 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$). We additionally explore agreement-aware pixel-level modulation as an exploratory extension; preliminary single-run results suggest modest boundary quality improvements (clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.6, BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8), though these require multi-seed validation to confirm.
    \item An empirical finding that Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) exhibits an \emph{asymmetric} effect in knowledge distillation: SRL benefits \emph{teacher} training (producing richer representations that improve student performance by +1.2 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$) but harms \emph{student} training when combined with DTKD ($-$1.1 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), revealing that optimal training recipes differ between teachers and distillation students.
    \item A comprehensive evaluation comprising six baselines (including SAM-LoRA and DINOv2-LoRA), per-tier difficulty analysis, multi-seed stability testing, and a feature-cluster split to assess visual near-duplicate leakage (train 631 / val 369 / test 500 images), demonstrating that task-specific design with knowledge distillation outperforms direct foundation-model fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets.
    \item Empirical analyses of training strategies and cross-domain transfer: loss exploration reveals a persistent crack--spalling tradeoff at shared class boundaries, and zero-shot evaluation on S2DS with component-level ablation shows that DSConv accounts for ${\sim}$80\% of the cross-domain mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Concrete defect segmentation}
\label{sec:related:defect}

Early CNN-based crack detectors such as CrackNet treated defect identification as patch classification \citep{zou2019deepcrack}.
Fully convolutional designs subsequently enabled dense pixel-wise prediction: DeepCrack \citep{liu2019deepcrack} introduced deeply-supervised side outputs, while \citet{zou2019deepcrack} exploited hierarchical features with multi-scale fusion.

Transformer-based architectures have demonstrated improved global context modelling.
CrackFormer \citep{liu2021crackformer} embeds self-attention to capture long-range crack continuity, and SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} provides a lightweight hierarchical encoder with an all-MLP decoder.
Universal architectures such as Mask2Former \citep{cheng2022mask2former} further push segmentation accuracy on general benchmarks but have seen limited adoption for infrastructure defects due to annotation scarcity.

Applied to dam inspection specifically, \citet{arafin2024concrete} performed multi-class concrete defect segmentation using DeepLabV3+, while \citet{li2024dam} proposed a lightweight CNN-Transformer hybrid for dam crack patrol inspection.
\citet{chen2024damsurvey} surveyed visual perception methods for intelligent dam inspection, identifying limited dataset scale and poor topology preservation as key open challenges.
More recently, CCT~Net \citep{gao2026cctnet} combined CNN and Transformer branches for dam surface crack segmentation, achieving 88.7\% mIoU on a custom single-class dataset, though it does not address multi-class segmentation or topology preservation.

\subsection{Topology-aware segmentation}
\label{sec:related:topology}

\citet{hu2019topology} introduced a differentiable topological loss based on persistent homology.
\citet{clough2020topological} extended persistent-homology supervision to cardiac imaging.
However, persistent-homology computation scales poorly to high-resolution images.

For tubular structures, \citet{shit2021cldice} proposed clDice, a differentiable soft-skeleton metric, but it requires GPU-based skeletonisation with substantial memory overhead.
The Skeleton Recall Loss \citep{shit2024skeleton} pre-computes ground-truth skeletons on CPU and formulates a recall objective, achieving superior connectivity with over 90\% memory reduction relative to clDice.
Recent work has critically examined topology-aware losses: \citet{arora2025srlcritique} question whether SRL's benefits generalise beyond the original evaluation setting, and \citet{joo2026cts} provide a faithful implementation of the Crack Topology Score with extensions for handling common prediction artifacts.

Our work evaluates SRL in the multi-class dam defect setting, revealing an asymmetric effect: SRL enriches teacher representations for distillation but harms student training when combined with soft-label objectives---consistent with the growing evidence that topology-aware losses require careful integration.

\subsection{Deformable and dynamic convolutions}
\label{sec:related:deformable}

Deformable Convolutional Networks (DCN) \citep{dai2017deformable} introduced learnable offsets for adaptive sampling.
Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} constrains offsets along a one-dimensional path, forcing snake-like sampling that traces elongated structures.
DSConv has since been adopted for various crack detection tasks: \citet{pan2025tunneldsconv} applied it to metro tunnel lining crack detection, and \citet{yu2024dscformer} proposed a dual-branch architecture combining enhanced DSConv with SegFormer for road crack segmentation, achieving competitive IoU on Crack3238 and FIND datasets.

Our work differs from \citet{yu2024dscformer} in three aspects: (1)~we address multi-class segmentation (crack \emph{and} spalling) rather than binary crack detection; (2)~we introduce dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD) with optional agreement-aware pixel-level modulation, which is absent from their architecture-only approach; and (3)~we target dam-specific inspection with topology-aware evaluation (ConnR, clDice) and cross-dataset transfer analysis.

\subsection{Knowledge distillation for segmentation}
\label{sec:related:kd}

Knowledge distillation (KD) \citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a smaller student by matching softened output distributions.
In semantic segmentation, structured KD approaches \citep{liu2019structured,wang2020intra} distil spatial relationships rather than just per-pixel logits.
Multi-teacher distillation \citep{you2017learning,yuan2021multihead} combines knowledge from multiple specialised teachers, but typically uses uniform teacher weighting across all classes.
\citet{zhang2022camkd} proposed confidence-aware multi-teacher KD (CA-MKD) with sample-wise teacher reliability weighting, though at the sample level rather than per-class or per-pixel. Our agreement-aware variant operates at the pixel level, using inter-teacher KL divergence rather than individual teacher confidence, which better captures spatial ambiguity at defect boundaries.
Recently, \citet{wang2025taskspecifickd} demonstrated task-specific KD from vision foundation models for medical segmentation, showing that distilling SAM's knowledge into lightweight students outperforms direct fine-tuning---a finding consistent with our results in the infrastructure domain.

Our dual-teacher strategy differs by combining a task-specific teacher with a foundation-model teacher whose complementary strengths are empirically validated, using per-class weighting in the teacher ensemble. Pixel-level agreement-aware modulation is explored as an extension.
This contrasts with CA-MKD's sample-level weighting and with \citet{wang2025taskspecifickd}'s single-teacher approach.

\subsection{Foundation models for segmentation}
\label{sec:related:foundation}

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} represent powerful vision foundation models.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} enables adaptation with minimal trainable parameters (${\sim}5\%$).
While promising on natural images, their effectiveness on small-scale industrial datasets remains underexplored.
\citet{ye2024samdefect} applied SAM-based instance segmentation for structural damage detection, demonstrating SAM's potential for infrastructure inspection but also noting the need for domain-specific adaptation.
We show that rather than directly deploying foundation models, using them as teachers in knowledge distillation yields better results---a strategy that preserves inference efficiency while inheriting the foundation model's structural knowledge.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the TopoDistill framework. Our approach enhances a SegFormer-B2 backbone at two complementary levels: (1)~\emph{feature extraction} via a parallel DSConv branch for geometry-aware crack sampling, and (2)~\emph{knowledge transfer} via dual-teacher distillation (DTKD) to combine the strengths of task-specific and foundation models.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig_architecture.png}
    \caption{Overview of the TopoDistill framework. (a)~A SegFormer-B2 encoder with a parallel CrackSnake branch (DSConv) produces 3-class segmentation logits; the DSConv output additively enhances only the crack channel. (b)~Dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD): Teacher~1 (TopoDistill, strong on pixel accuracy) and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA, strong on structural connectivity) are combined into a per-class weighted ensemble. A pixel-level agreement map $a(i)$, derived from teacher disagreement, optionally modulates the KD loss so the student learns strongly where teachers agree and falls back on ground-truth supervision at ambiguous pixels.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{SegFormer-B2 backbone}

We adopt SegFormer-B2 \citep{xie2021segformer} as our backbone, providing a hierarchical Mix Transformer (MiT) encoder with four stages at resolutions $H/4$, $H/8$, $H/16$, and $H/32$ with channel dimensions 64, 128, 320, and 512. The all-MLP decoder fuses multi-scale features via linear projection and upsampling. We initialise from weights pretrained on ADE20K at $512{\times}512$.

\subsection{Dynamic Snake Convolution branch}
\label{sec:dsconv}

Standard convolutions sample on fixed rectangular grids---poorly matched to thin, curvilinear cracks. Inspired by \citet{qi2023dynamic}, we introduce a parallel \emph{CrackSnakeBranch} that performs deformable sampling along crack geometry.

\paragraph{Architecture.} The branch consumes encoder stages 1 and 2 (at $H/4$ and $H/8$ resolution). Two parallel DSConv paths deform sampling along the $x$-axis and $y$-axis respectively, each with kernel size $K{=}9$ and hidden dimension $d{=}64$. The outputs are projected to a single-channel crack enhancement map $\mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$.

\paragraph{Additive enhancement.} The map is added \emph{only} to the crack channel logits:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{crack}} = \mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}} + \mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}}
    \label{eq:dsconv}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}}$ denotes the decoder's crack logits. Spalling and background logits remain unmodified.

\paragraph{Zero-initialisation.} The output projection is initialised to zero, so the branch starts as a no-op and gradually learns crack-specific deformations during training.

\subsection{Dual-teacher knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:dtkd}

A key observation from our experiments is that different model architectures excel at different quality dimensions: the task-specific TopoDistill (Teacher~1) achieves higher pixel-level accuracy (IoU), while the foundation-model SAM-LoRA (Teacher~2) achieves higher structural connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$), driven primarily by its substantially stronger spalling connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$ 86.0\% vs.\ 73.4\%; Table~\ref{tab:foundation}). Standard single-teacher distillation cannot exploit this complementarity.

We propose \emph{dual-teacher knowledge distillation} (DTKD) that constructs an ensemble of teacher predictions:

\paragraph{Teacher models.} We use two pretrained teachers, both trained with Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} to enrich their learned representations (the rationale for this choice is validated in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Teacher~1} ($T_1$): TopoDistill (task-specific, strong on pixel accuracy)
    \item \textbf{Teacher~2} ($T_2$): SAM-LoRA (foundation model, strong on structural connectivity)
\end{itemize}
We select SAM-LoRA over DINOv2-LoRA as Teacher~2 because SAM-LoRA achieves substantially higher ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ (81.2\% vs.\ 76.5\%) and BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ (68.1\% vs.\ 62.4\%), providing complementary structural connectivity knowledge (Table~\ref{tab:main}). DINOv2-LoRA's advantage is limited to IoU$_{\text{sp}}$, which Teacher~1 already covers.

\paragraph{Teacher ensemble.} For each class $c$, the ensemble teacher probability is computed as a weighted combination:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{p}_c = w_{1,c} \cdot p_{1,c} + w_{2,c} \cdot p_{2,c}
    \label{eq:dtkd_ensemble}
\end{equation}
where $p_{i,c} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_i / \tau)_c$ is the softened probability from teacher $i$ at temperature $\tau$, and $w_{1,c} + w_{2,c} = 1$ for each class.

The DTKD configuration used throughout this paper assigns manually chosen per-class weights: $w_{2,\text{cr}} = 0.6$, $w_{2,\text{sp}} = 0.3$, $w_{2,\text{bg}} = 0.5$, where $w_2$ denotes Teacher~2's contribution.
These values were selected during early development to emphasise Teacher~2's structural knowledge for crack and Teacher~1's pixel accuracy for spalling; however, no systematic search was performed, and we do not claim these are optimal.
As Table~\ref{tab:kd_weights} shows, equal weighting achieves nearly identical mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ (72.2 vs.\ 72.3), confirming that DTKD's primary value lies in the dual-teacher ensemble itself rather than in the specific weight assignment.

\paragraph{Distillation loss.} The ensemble probabilities are renormalised to sum to one and the student is trained with:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = \tau^2 \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{p}} \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_s / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:kd_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ denotes the renormalised class-conditional ensemble probability and $\mathbf{z}_s$ the student logits.

\paragraph{Agreement-aware distillation.}
When two teachers with different architectures and training recipes are combined, their predictions inevitably disagree at certain pixels---particularly at ambiguous crack--background boundaries and thin structures. At such pixels the ensemble soft label is unreliable, and forcing the student to match it may introduce noise. We note that high inter-teacher agreement does not guarantee correctness---both teachers may agree on an incorrect label---but empirically, agreement-weighted pixels yield more consistent supervision than disagreement regions.
We quantify per-pixel teacher agreement using the KL divergence between the two softened teacher distributions:
\begin{equation}
    d(i) = \text{KL}\!\left(\text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{T_1}^{(i)} / \tau) \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{T_2}^{(i)} / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:teacher_disagree}
\end{equation}
where $i$ indexes spatial positions. An agreement map is derived as $a(i) = 1 - d(i) / \max_j d(j)$, ranging from 1 (full agreement) to 0 (maximum disagreement). The agreement-aware distillation loss modulates the per-pixel KD contribution:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}^{\text{conf}} = \tau^2 \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i} a(i) \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(i)} \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_s^{(i)} / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:conf_kd}
\end{equation}
This ensures the student learns strongly from the ensemble where both teachers concur, but falls back on the ground-truth supervised loss at ambiguous pixels. The agreement map is computed within the existing forward pass (no additional inference cost).
We note that this formulation uses per-image max-normalisation, which makes the agreement signal sensitive to outlier disagreement values and not directly comparable across samples. Alternative designs (e.g., JS divergence, exponential decay, or fixed-threshold gating) may address these limitations but are left to future work.
Fig.~\ref{fig:agreement} visualises the disagreement map on representative Hard-tier samples: high-disagreement pixels (bright regions) concentrate at crack--background boundaries and thin branching points, precisely where the ensemble soft label is least reliable.
We empirically validate that teacher disagreement correlates with sample difficulty: the mean per-sample KL divergence between teachers is 43\% higher on Hard-tier images than on Easy-tier images (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Total loss.} The primary training objective (row~c) balances the segmentation loss and distillation loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha) \left[\lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}\right] + \alpha \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$, and $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 0.5$.
The agreement-aware variant (row~g) substitutes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}^{\text{conf}}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:conf_kd}) for $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}$, down-weighting unreliable pixels.
Note that SRL is used only during \emph{teacher} training; the student does not use SRL, as we find it conflicts with the distillation objective (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Test-time augmentation}

At inference, we optionally employ multi-scale TTA: each image is evaluated at three scales (0.75$\times$, 1.0$\times$, 1.25$\times$) with horizontal and vertical flips (9 views). Logits are averaged before argmax.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset}

We evaluate on the \textbf{semantic segmentation subset} of the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, which comprises 3{,}500 image patches captured during an on-site visit of a concrete dam, supporting classification, detection, and segmentation tasks.
The segmentation subset contains 1{,}500 pixel-level annotated images with three classes (background, crack, spalling) at $512{\times}512$ resolution, organised into three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard; 500 each) based on crack detection difficulty, enabling curriculum learning.
We split the data into train/val/test sets of 1{,}200/150/150 images, stratified by tier. Splitting is at the patch level; patches from adjacent regions may share visual characteristics (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset} shows representative samples from each tier with ground-truth overlays; crack pixels range from 0.3\% to 7.9\% and spalling from 1.0\% to 11.7\%, illustrating the extreme class imbalance.

\subsection{Implementation details}

All models are trained on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU using PyTorch.
TopoDistill uses SegFormer-B2 pretrained on ADE20K-512, trained for 100 epochs with AdamW (lr $6{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), 5-epoch warmup, batch size 4.
The DSConv branch uses $d{=}64$, $K{=}9$.
Data augmentation consists of random horizontal and vertical flips ($p{=}0.5$ each); images are resized to $512{\times}512$ and normalised with ImageNet statistics.

For DTKD, Teacher~1 (TopoDistill+SRL) and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA+SRL) are trained independently first with SRL ($\lambda_3 = 0.05$, activated at epoch 60), then frozen during student training.
The student has identical architecture to Teacher~1 but is trained \emph{without} SRL (CE + Dice + KD only).
Distillation uses $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$.

For foundation model baselines, SAM-LoRA uses SAM's ViT-B encoder with LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha$=32); we replace SAM's original prompt-conditioned mask decoder with a lightweight FPN decoder for prompt-free semantic segmentation, so no prompts (ground-truth-derived or otherwise) are used at training or test time. DINOv2-LoRA adapts ViT-B/14 similarly with input size $518{\times}518$ (patch-size multiple). Both train for 50 epochs with batch size 2.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

We report complementary metrics capturing different aspects of segmentation quality:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$}: Mean IoU of crack and spalling classes.
    \item \textbf{IoU$_{\text{cr}}$}, \textbf{IoU$_{\text{sp}}$}: Per-class IoU.
    \item \textbf{BF1}: Boundary F1 score (2-pixel tolerance) \citep{csurka2013good}.
    \item \textbf{clDice}: Centreline Dice via skeletonisation \citep{shit2021cldice}.
    \item \textbf{ConnR}: Connectivity Ratio---fraction of GT connected components with $\geq$50\% overlap in prediction, following the component-level evaluation protocol of \citet{shit2021cldice}. The 50\% threshold balances sensitivity to fragmentation with robustness to minor boundary shifts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} summarises the model complexity and inference speed measured on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU at each model's native input resolution.
TopoDistill adds 4.0M parameters (+14.7\%) over SegFormer-B2 due to the DSConv branch, with a modest FPS reduction (124$\to$96).
Crucially, DTKD incurs \emph{no additional inference cost}: the two teachers are only used during training, and the student has identical architecture to the base TopoDistill.
Mask2Former is the heaviest model (68.7M, 39 FPS), while foundation model baselines (SAM-LoRA, DINOv2-LoRA) carry $>$90M parameters due to their ViT-B encoders, though only ${\sim}$5--6M are trainable via LoRA.

\paragraph{(a$'$) vs.\ (b): DSConv's geometric bias.}
To verify that DSConv's gains stem from its deformable sampling geometry rather than additional capacity, we compare it against a parameter-matched standard Conv branch (row~a$'$, ${\sim}$1.3M parameters, using directional $1{\times}K$ and $K{\times}1$ convolutions without deformable sampling). Note: the DSConv branch itself contains ${\sim}$1.3M parameters; the 4.0M total overhead reported in Table~\ref{tab:cost} includes the additional decode head parameters needed to fuse the branch output with the SegFormer decoder.
Standard Conv \emph{degrades} performance relative to vanilla SegFormer: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ drops from 70.3 to 66.2 ($-$4.1), with IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ falling sharply ($-$7.0).
In contrast, DSConv (row~b) with the same parameter budget improves connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ +2.8, clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +3.0) while maintaining mIoU.
The ${\sim}$4-point gap between rows (a$'$) and (b) confirms that DSConv's value lies in its adaptive deformable sampling, not in the extra parameters.

\paragraph{(a)$\to$(b): Adding DSConv.}
The DSConv branch produces minimal mIoU change (+0.1) but substantially improves crack connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ increases by \textbf{+2.8} (81.0$\to$83.8) and clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ by \textbf{+3.0} (82.7$\to$85.7), along with improved boundary quality (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.0, BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ +2.0).
The deformable snake-shaped sampling captures elongated crack geometry that fixed rectangular kernels miss, demonstrating that DSConv's primary value lies in its geometric inductive bias for connectivity.

\paragraph{(b$_1$)--(b$_2$) vs.\ (c): Single- vs.\ dual-teacher KD.}
To verify that both teachers are necessary, we train single-teacher KD variants with identical student settings as row~(c): row~(b$_1$) uses only Teacher~1 (task-specific) and row~(b$_2$) uses only Teacher~2 (foundation model), with the other teacher's weight set to zero.
Neither single teacher matches the dual-teacher result: T1-only achieves 69.0\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ and T2-only 69.2\%, both ${\sim}$3 points below dual-teacher DTKD (72.3\%) and even below the no-KD DSConv baseline (row~b, 70.4\%).
This indicates that a single teacher's soft labels, occupying 50\% of the training signal ($\alpha{=}0.5$), can introduce more noise than useful information when the teacher lacks complementary knowledge to counterbalance its biases.
The two teachers nonetheless show complementary strengths: T2-only achieves substantially higher ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ (85.0 vs.\ T1-only's 81.0), reflecting SAM-LoRA's stronger structural connectivity knowledge, while both show similar IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ (${\sim}$56).
Dual-teacher KD combines these complementary strengths, achieving the best mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ and IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ while maintaining strong crack metrics.

\paragraph{(b)$\to$(c): Adding DTKD.}
DTKD provides substantial pixel-level improvement: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.1, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +2.8, clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.5, and BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.6, while also improving overall connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +2.6 (80.4$\to$83.0).
ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ decreases modestly from 83.8 to 82.1 ($-$1.7), as DTKD's soft-label supervision slightly smooths fine crack structures, but this is more than offset by substantially improved spalling connectivity.
The dual-teacher ensemble ensures that the student receives complementary guidance from both teacher architectures (Section~\ref{sec:dtkd}).

\paragraph{(c)$\to$(g): Agreement-aware KD (exploratory).}
Replacing the standard KD loss with agreement-aware distillation (Eq.~\ref{eq:conf_kd}) shows boundary quality improvements in a single run: BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8 (75.6$\to$76.4) and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.6 (83.3$\to$83.9), with mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.2.
However, the mIoU gain (+0.2) falls within the multi-seed standard deviation of DTKD (0.52, Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}), and ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ decreases (82.1$\to$81.4, $-$0.7). Without multi-seed validation, these improvements should be interpreted cautiously.
The mechanism---down-weighting the distillation signal at high-disagreement pixels---is principled, but the current agreement map formulation (per-image max-normalisation) has limitations: sensitivity to outlier disagreement values and incomparability across samples.

To isolate the contribution of agreement-aware KD from difficulty-guided sample reweighting (an alternative use of teacher disagreement), we conducted an ablation using disagreement-based per-sample loss reweighting \emph{without} pixel-level confidence modulation.
This variant degraded performance (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 72.1, BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ 70.0), indicating that on small datasets, per-sample reweighting introduces training instability, while pixel-level modulation of the distillation signal is the effective mechanism.

Analysis of teacher disagreement across difficulty tiers reveals that the mean per-sample KL divergence between teachers is 0.046 on Hard-tier images vs.\ 0.032 on Easy-tier images (+43\%), confirming that disagreement is an informative proxy for sample difficulty.
However, this correlation does not translate into effective sample-level curriculum learning---consistent with prior findings that data-restricting or reweighting curriculum strategies underperform on small datasets where every sample matters.

\paragraph{(c)$\to$(d): Test-time augmentation.}
TTA provides modest additional gains (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.4, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8) but reduces connectivity metrics (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 83.0$\to$80.2), suggesting that multi-scale/flip aggregation improves pixel coverage but may smooth fine structural details.

\paragraph{Effect of SRL (rows e--f).}
\label{sec:srl}
Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} penalises low predicted probability at ground-truth skeleton pixels $\mathcal{S}$, precomputed offline via morphological skeletonisation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SRL}} = 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{S}} \hat{y}_{\text{crack}}(p)
    \label{eq:srl}
\end{equation}
We apply SRL only to the crack class (activated at epoch 60, weight $\lambda_3 = 0.05$).
Rows (e)--(f) show the effect of adding SRL to the pipeline.
Adding SRL to DSConv (row e) provides no crack improvement (IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ $-$0.1, BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ $-$0.3) and damages connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ drops from 80.4 to 77.8 ($-$2.6), driven by ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$ falling from 77.0 to 73.4 as SRL's crack-focused supervision fragments spalling components at ambiguous boundaries.
Adding DTKD on top of SRL (row f) partially recovers connectivity (77.8$\to$80.6) but the resulting model (f, mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 71.2\%) underperforms the SRL-free pipeline (c, mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 72.3\%) by \textbf{$-$1.1}.
This demonstrates that SRL's gradient interference with DTKD's soft-label supervision is a net negative: the student learns better from teacher signals alone without the competing skeleton objective.
Multi-seed experiments (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}) further confirm that the SRL path is unstable (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ std 2.42 vs.\ 0.70 for DSConv-only and 0.52 for DTKD).

\paragraph{Overall progression.}
From baseline (a) to the primary method DSConv+DTKD (c), the cumulative improvement is +2.0 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, +1.7 BF1$_{\text{fg}}$, +2.8 clDice$_{\text{fg}}$, and +5.6 ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$; DSConv drives connectivity while DTKD contributes pixel accuracy on this split (see Table~\ref{tab:group_split} for split-dependence analysis). The exploratory agreement-aware extension (g) adds modest boundary gains (+0.8 BF1$_{\text{cr}}$) that require multi-seed confirmation.

\paragraph{Statistical significance.}
We conduct paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the test images, comparing DSConv-only (row~b) vs.\ DSConv+DTKD (row~c, final model).
Seven of ten per-class metrics reach \emph{uncorrected} significance at $\alpha{=}0.05$: six show DTKD improvement---IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.008$), Dice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.007$), BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.046$), clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{<}0.001$), IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ ($p{=}0.016$), and Dice$_{\text{sp}}$ ($p{=}0.016$)---while ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ shows a significant decrease ($-$1.7, $p{=}0.040$), reflecting the connectivity trade-off discussed above.
After Holm--Bonferroni correction for 10 simultaneous comparisons, only clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p_{\text{adj}}{=}0.010$) remains significant at the family-wise $\alpha{=}0.05$ level; the remaining metrics show adjusted $p$-values between 0.063 and 0.30. The results consistently favour DTKD across several metrics, but the core measures (mIoU, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$) do not survive multiple-comparison correction. Therefore, while the directional consistency is encouraging, we cannot claim statistically confirmed gains on these metrics based on the current test set.
Tier-stratified analysis reveals the strongest effects on Hard images: IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.003$), Dice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.004$), BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.045$), and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{<}0.001$) all reach uncorrected significance, suggesting that the foundation-model teacher's global knowledge provides the strongest benefit on complex crack patterns.

The majority of false positives occur at crack--background boundaries, where thin crack predictions leak into adjacent background pixels.
This precision bottleneck motivates exploring loss functions that penalise false positives more heavily.

\paragraph{Crack--spalling tradeoff.}
A consistent pattern emerges across all loss-based strategies: improving crack boundary precision degrades spalling metrics.
Tversky loss with $\alpha{=}0.6$ (stronger FP penalty) improves BF1$_{\text{sp}}$ by +3.4 over the baseline, but reduces mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ by $-$0.6 and ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ by $-$3.6.
WABL, which specifically targets thin-crack boundary false positives, causes the largest spalling regression (IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$1.4, BF1$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$1.6).
Combining WABL with Tversky amplifies the degradation (IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$2.8).
This tradeoff arises because crack and spalling often co-occur at shared boundaries; loss terms that suppress crack false positives inadvertently penalise adjacent spalling predictions.

\paragraph{Hard-tier fine-tuning.}
Sampling-based approaches partially mitigate this tradeoff.
Fine-tuning from the DTKD checkpoint with 70\% Hard-tier oversampling and WABL achieves BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ 72.2 (comparable to the best loss-based variant) while maintaining better spalling coverage (IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ 85.0 vs.\ 83.4--85.4 for loss variants) and the highest clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ (87.3).
However, no strategy surpasses the base DTKD model on mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ (72.3), indicating that the standard CE+Dice combination is already near-optimal for this dataset and that the crack precision bottleneck requires fundamentally different approaches (e.g., higher-resolution inputs or explicit boundary modelling in the architecture).

\paragraph{Teacher selection ablation.}
Since the DTKD student does not use SRL, one might expect a teacher trained \emph{without} SRL (higher ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$) to provide cleaner knowledge transfer.
We test this by replacing Teacher~1 (TopoDistill+SRL, ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 77.8\%) with the SRL-free DSConv-only variant (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 80.4\%).
The resulting student achieves 71.1\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, \textbf{$-$1.2} below the G1 teacher variant (72.3\%).
The degradation concentrates on spalling (IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$2.4, ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$1.4), while crack metrics are largely unaffected.
This indicates that SRL-trained Teacher~1 produces more useful soft supervision for the student---despite its own connectivity regression---that transfers more effectively through distillation.
Teacher quality for KD is not simply a function of the teacher's own test metrics; the diversity of learned features matters more.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{DTKD: benefits and limitations.}
On the original split, DTKD adds +1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ over DSConv alone. The full pipeline (DSConv+DTKD) reduces cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3$\times$ vs.\ the SegFormer baseline (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ std 0.52 vs.\ 1.47, Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}). However, DSConv alone already achieves most of this reduction (std 0.70); DTKD's additional contribution to stability (0.70$\to$0.52) is marginal given only three seeds. Furthermore, the mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain does not replicate on the feature-cluster split ($-$0.6, Table~\ref{tab:group_split}), and the multi-seed means differ by only 1.0 point (DSConv-only 70.8\% vs.\ DSConv+DTKD 71.8\%), a more modest gap than the single-seed +1.9 suggests. DTKD's clearest contribution is in cross-dataset transfer (+1.6 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ on S2DS, atop SRL's +2.2). We note that the stability observations are based on three seeds and do not constitute evidence for a specific mechanism (e.g., loss landscape smoothing); additional seeds and loss landscape analysis would be needed to support mechanistic claims.

\paragraph{DTKD hyperparameter sensitivity check.}
We performed a star search over the two key DTKD hyperparameters: softmax temperature $\tau \in \{2, 4, 6\}$ (fixing $\alpha{=}0.5$) and KD loss weight $\alpha \in \{0.3, 0.5, 0.7\}$ (fixing $\tau{=}4$), where $\alpha$ is the distillation weight in Eq.~\eqref{eq:total_loss}. Table~\ref{tab:hyper} reports validation-set mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ (used for checkpoint selection) for each configuration (single run; different random seeds). Lower temperature ($\tau{=}2$) produced sharper teacher signals that degraded val mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ to 69.7\%; higher temperature ($\tau{=}6$) achieved 71.4\%, marginally above the default's 71.1\%; shifting $\alpha$ in either direction yielded 70.2--71.0\%. The default and $\tau{=}6$ are within 0.3\%, well within the seed noise observed in Table~\ref{tab:multiseed} (std $\approx$ 0.5), so we retain the commonly used $\tau{=}4$ without claiming optimality. Only $\tau{=}2$ shows a clear degradation ($-$1.4\%), suggesting that excessively sharp teacher signals hurt. Overall, the results indicate reasonable local robustness within the examined range, though the search covers only five points and each is a single run with a different random seed.

\paragraph{Granularity of difficulty-aware modulation.}
Inter-teacher disagreement provides a free quality signal that correlates with sample difficulty (+43\% KL divergence on Hard vs.\ Easy). Leveraging this signal at the \emph{pixel level} (agreement-aware KD) shows promising single-run results on boundary metrics, while using it for \emph{sample-level} curriculum learning degrades performance. This suggests that on small datasets, every sample is needed for generalisation, but noisy pixels within a sample may be safely down-weighted---though multi-seed validation is needed to confirm that the pixel-level gains exceed random variation.

\paragraph{SRL: useful for teachers, harmful for students.}
SRL leads the teacher to produce more useful soft supervision that transfers well through distillation, even though SRL degrades the teacher's own connectivity metrics. As a student training component, however, SRL interferes with DTKD's soft-label supervision. This asymmetry---that optimal teacher and student training recipes differ---has broader implications for distillation pipeline design.

\paragraph{Indirect use of foundation models.}
Rather than directly fine-tuning SAM or DINOv2 (which underperforms task-specific architectures by 2--4\% mIoU on small datasets), using them as \emph{teachers} in knowledge distillation yields better results. The student inherits the foundation model's structural knowledge while maintaining inference efficiency identical to the base model.

\paragraph{Crack--spalling tradeoff.}
The persistent tradeoff across all loss-based strategies (Section~\ref{sec:strategy}) arises from shared geometric boundaries between defect types. Pixel-level loss modification alone cannot resolve the crack precision bottleneck without collateral damage to spalling, suggesting that sampling-based, architectural, or post-processing approaches (e.g., class-specific CRF refinement) are more promising.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
The student model has identical inference cost to the base TopoDistill (96 FPS, 31.3M parameters), with teachers used only during training. On the original split, the per-tier analysis shows DTKD improves all tiers, with the largest mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain on Easy (+3.4) and the strongest crack-specific significance on Hard (uncorrected $p$-values), though the overall DTKD gain exhibits split dependence (Table~\ref{tab:group_split}).

\paragraph{Failure modes and limitations.}
The model underperforms on (i)~thin hairline cracks (${<}$2 pixels wide) and (ii)~co-occurring crack--spalling regions where it over-predicts one class at the expense of the other.
The DamSegment dataset comprises images from a single dam site with patch-level splitting; spatially adjacent patches may share visual characteristics, potentially inflating in-domain metrics. To assess visual near-duplicate leakage, we constructed a \emph{feature-cluster split} after all design decisions were locked on the original split: ResNet-50 features were extracted for all 1{,}500 patches, agglomerative clustering (cosine distance, 100 groups) ensured visually similar patches stay in the same fold, and per-tier stratification preserved the Easy/Medium/Hard ratio (${\approx}$42/25/33 train/val/test). The cluster test set was not observed during model design or hyperparameter tuning. All models were retrained from scratch on this split and evaluated on the corresponding test set. Table~\ref{tab:group_split} compares these results against the original split. Since the two test sets differ in size and composition, modest absolute differences are expected; the key diagnostic is whether feature-cluster split performance drops \emph{substantially} (which would signal near-duplicate leakage). Across all six models, performance is within $\pm$3\% of the original, and five of six score \emph{higher}---suggesting that near-duplicate patches between train and test are not the primary driver of the reported gains.
We note that this feature-cluster split addresses \emph{visual} near-duplicates but not true \emph{spatial} leakage: patch coordinates, source image IDs, and dam-face regions were unavailable, so spatially adjacent patches with different visual appearances may still span train and test. A coordinate-based or source-image-based split would provide stronger evidence; absent such metadata, this experiment should be interpreted as a partial check rather than a definitive spatial leakage assessment.
The S2DS cross-dataset transfer further corroborates this conclusion but reveals a substantial domain gap (${\sim}$19\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), indicating that zero-shot cross-site transfer remains far from solved. Multi-site datasets and domain adaptation would strengthen generalisability.

\paragraph{Test-set exposure during development.}
All ablation variants were evaluated on a single held-out test set. Although a separate validation set was used for hyperparameter selection and early stopping, test-set metrics were observed during model development across ablation rows. This means that design decisions---including module selection, teacher selection, loss design, and per-class weight assignment---may have been indirectly influenced by test-set performance, compromising its independence. The reported test-set differences between ablation rows (particularly small gains such as mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.1--0.2) should therefore be interpreted as upper bounds rather than unbiased estimates.
All architectural and training decisions were finalised on the original split before the feature-cluster split was constructed; the cluster test set (500 images) was not observed during model design or hyperparameter tuning. The feature-cluster replication (Table~\ref{tab:group_split}) shows that all retrained models remain stable under this split, with no substantial performance degradation (all within $\pm$3\% of original). Critically, the DSConv-only comparator (row~b) was also retrained on this split: DSConv achieves 73.3\% vs.\ DTKD's 72.7\% on the cluster test set, meaning the +1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ advantage observed on the original split does \emph{not} replicate ($-$0.6 on the cluster split). This reversal is within the seed noise range (std $\approx$ 0.5, Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}), but it cautions against interpreting the original-split DTKD gain as a robust finding; the incremental benefit of distillation over DSConv alone may be smaller than the original split suggests. The multi-seed analysis (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}) shows that DSConv-only and DSConv+DTKD differ by only 1.0 point in mean mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ (70.8\% vs.\ 71.8\%) with comparable low variance (std 0.70 vs.\ 0.52), further suggesting that DTKD's incremental contribution is more modest than the single-seed +1.9 implies.
Nonetheless, a fully rigorous protocol would require locking all design decisions on the validation set and evaluating the final model on a previously unobserved test split, or reporting repeated cross-validation with confidence intervals. We did not adopt this protocol, which is a limitation shared with most segmentation studies on fixed benchmarks but nonetheless weakens the causal interpretation of incremental ablation gains.
Per-class teacher weights are set manually; adaptive weighting is future work.
Crack IoU remains challenging (${\sim}$59\%) due to extreme class imbalance, and the DSConv branch is crack-specific.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented TopoDistill, a topology-aware segmentation framework for concrete dam defects that combines Dynamic Snake Convolution with dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD).
DSConv provides robust geometric inductive bias for crack connectivity across both the original and feature-cluster splits, and is the primary source of both cross-seed stability (std 1.47$\to$0.70) and cross-dataset transfer (${\sim}$80\% of the S2DS gain). SRL and DTKD together contribute a further +3.8 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ on S2DS (SRL +2.2, DTKD +1.6), with DTKD also providing marginal additional variance reduction (std 0.70$\to$0.52), though its mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain over DSConv alone exhibits split dependence (+1.9 on the original split, $-$0.6 on the cluster split). Together the two enhancements achieve 72.3\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ and 83.0\% ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ on DamSegment at no additional inference cost. A preliminary agreement-aware distillation extension shows promising boundary quality improvements in single-run evaluation (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8), warranting future multi-seed validation.
An investigation of Skeleton Recall Loss revealed an asymmetric effect in distillation---SRL benefits teachers but harms students---highlighting that optimal training recipes differ across pipeline stages.
Exploration of alternative loss functions uncovered a persistent crack--spalling tradeoff at shared class boundaries. Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS with component-level ablation showed that DSConv is the primary driver of cross-domain robustness, accounting for ${\sim}$80\% of the mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain (0.1\%$\to$14.9\%), with SRL and DTKD contributing a further +3.8 (to 18.7\%).
These findings demonstrate that indirectly leveraging foundation models through knowledge distillation is more effective than direct fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets, providing practical guidance for data-scarce inspection scenarios.
