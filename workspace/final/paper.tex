\begin{abstract}
Automated semantic segmentation of cracks and spalling on concrete dam surfaces is essential for structural health monitoring. While pixel-level metrics such as IoU capture overall coverage, they do not guarantee the \emph{structural continuity} of detected defects---a fragmented crack prediction may achieve adequate IoU while failing to reveal the true crack path, leading to underestimated severity in safety assessment. Existing methods optimise pixel-level objectives without explicitly preserving this continuity.
We propose \textbf{DSCFormer}, a topology-aware segmentation framework incorporating two complementary enhancements to a SegFormer-B2 backbone:
(1)~a \emph{Dynamic Snake Convolution} (DSConv) branch that captures elongated crack geometry through deformable sampling, adding only 4M parameters (+14.5\%); and
(2)~\emph{Class-Conditional Dual-Teacher Knowledge Distillation} (DTKD) that transfers complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher with per-class weighting and pixel-level confidence modulation based on teacher agreement, \emph{at zero additional inference cost}.
On the \textbf{DamSegment} dataset \citep{damsegment2026} (1,500 images, 3~classes), DSCFormer+DTKD achieves 72.3\% foreground mIoU (72.7\% with TTA), the highest among all compared methods, with 86.4\% centreline Dice.
Ablation experiments confirm complementary gains: DSConv drives crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6), while DTKD drives pixel-level accuracy (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9 over DSConv alone) at a modest cost to crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ $-$2.6), with improvements concentrated on Hard-tier images.
We also investigate Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL), finding that while it improves crack boundary accuracy in isolation, it degrades the overall pipeline when combined with DTKD ($-$1.1 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), revealing a negative interaction between skeleton-level supervision and soft-label distillation.
Multi-seed experiments reveal that DTKD also acts as a strong regulariser, reducing cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3$\times$ compared to the baseline (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ std: 0.52 vs.\ 1.47).
Class-conditional teacher weighting provides marginal gains over equal weighting (+0.1 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$) but improves crack centreline preservation (clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.6).
Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS demonstrates that DSCFormer+DTKD achieves 18.7\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, compared to 0.1\% for SegFormer-B2 and 2.5\% for Mask2Former, indicating that the geometric inductive bias substantially improves cross-domain robustness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Concrete dams are safety-critical infrastructure whose structural integrity must be continuously monitored throughout multi-decade service lives.
Surface defects---predominantly cracks and spalling---serve as early indicators of internal deterioration, yet their manual inspection remains labour-intensive, subjective, and logistically constrained by the scale and accessibility of dam faces \citep{chen2024damsurvey,fang2024dam}.
Automated semantic segmentation of these defects from high-resolution imagery therefore promises substantial gains in inspection efficiency and repeatability.

Recent deep-learning methods have advanced pixel-level defect segmentation considerably.
Encoder--decoder architectures such as U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} and hierarchical transformers like SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} achieve competitive Intersection-over-Union (IoU) on benchmark crack datasets \citep{zou2019deepcrack,liu2019deepcrack}.
However, IoU and related region-overlap metrics are insensitive to topological errors: a prediction that fragments a continuous crack into disconnected segments may still score favourably on IoU while misrepresenting the structural extent of the defect \citep{pantoja2024cracktopology}.
For structural safety assessment, the \emph{connectivity} of a predicted crack is as important as its pixel coverage---a fragmented prediction can lead an engineer to underestimate propagation length and thus misjudge residual capacity.

Topology-preserving losses such as clDice \citep{shit2021cldice} and persistent-homology penalties \citep{hu2019topology,clough2020topological} partially address this gap, yet they either incur prohibitive GPU memory for high-resolution dam images or operate on binary masks without multi-class support.
The recently proposed Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} overcomes both limitations by computing recall on CPU-extracted ground-truth skeletons with over 90\% reduction in computational overhead.
Concurrently, Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} reshapes kernel sampling grids to follow elongated, tortuous paths---a morphological prior that aligns naturally with the geometry of cracks.

Meanwhile, large vision foundation models such as SAM \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} offer powerful pretrained representations, yet our experiments show that parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} on small industrial datasets underperforms fully fine-tuned task-specific architectures.
A natural question arises: \emph{can the complementary strengths of task-specific and foundation models be combined}?
We address this through knowledge distillation, where a student model learns from both a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher with class-conditional weighting.

In this paper we propose \textbf{DSCFormer}, a framework that augments SegFormer-B2 with a parallel DSConv branch and class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD). We additionally investigate Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) and uncover an asymmetric role in distillation: SRL enriches teacher training but harms the student when combined with DTKD.
We evaluate on the semantic segmentation subset of the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, comprising 1,500 annotated dam surface images.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A topology-aware segmentation framework with two complementary enhancements: a parallel Dynamic Snake Convolution branch that drives crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6) through geometry-aware sampling, and a dual-teacher knowledge distillation scheme (DTKD) that drives pixel-level accuracy (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9 over DSConv alone) by transferring complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher. DTKD incorporates confidence-aware distillation that modulates the KD loss at pixel level based on teacher agreement, improving boundary quality (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8, clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.6) at no additional inference cost. The framework adds only 4M parameters with no inference-time overhead from distillation.
    \item An empirical finding that Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) exhibits an \emph{asymmetric} effect in knowledge distillation: SRL benefits \emph{teacher} training (producing richer representations that improve student performance by +1.2 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$) but harms \emph{student} training when combined with DTKD ($-$1.1 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), revealing that optimal training recipes differ between teachers and distillation students.
    \item A comprehensive evaluation including comparison with six baselines (including SAM-LoRA and DINOv2-LoRA), per-tier difficulty analysis, statistical significance testing, and zero-shot cross-dataset transfer on S2DS, demonstrating that task-specific design with knowledge distillation outperforms direct foundation-model fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets and that DSConv's geometric inductive bias substantially improves cross-domain robustness.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Concrete defect segmentation}
\label{sec:related:defect}

Early CNN-based crack detectors such as CrackNet treated defect identification as patch classification \citep{zou2019deepcrack}.
Fully convolutional designs subsequently enabled dense pixel-wise prediction: DeepCrack \citep{liu2019deepcrack} introduced deeply-supervised side outputs, while \citet{zou2019deepcrack} exploited hierarchical features with multi-scale fusion.

Transformer-based architectures have demonstrated improved global context modelling.
CrackFormer \citep{liu2021crackformer} embeds self-attention to capture long-range crack continuity, and SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} provides a lightweight hierarchical encoder with an all-MLP decoder.
Universal architectures such as Mask2Former \citep{cheng2022mask2former} further push segmentation accuracy on general benchmarks but have seen limited adoption for infrastructure defects due to annotation scarcity.

Applied to dam inspection specifically, \citet{arafin2024concrete} performed multi-class concrete defect segmentation using DeepLabV3+, while \citet{fang2024dam} proposed a lightweight architecture for microscopic dam crack quantification.
\citet{chen2024damsurvey} surveyed visual perception methods for intelligent dam inspection, identifying limited dataset scale and poor topology preservation as key open challenges.

\subsection{Topology-aware segmentation}
\label{sec:related:topology}

\citet{hu2019topology} introduced a differentiable topological loss based on persistent homology.
\citet{clough2020topological} extended persistent-homology supervision to cardiac imaging.
However, persistent-homology computation scales poorly to high-resolution images.

For tubular structures, \citet{shit2021cldice} proposed clDice, a differentiable soft-skeleton metric, but it requires GPU-based skeletonisation with substantial memory overhead.
The Skeleton Recall Loss \citep{shit2024skeleton} pre-computes ground-truth skeletons on CPU and formulates a recall objective, achieving superior connectivity with over 90\% memory reduction relative to clDice.

Our work evaluates SRL in the multi-class dam defect setting, revealing an asymmetric effect: SRL enriches teacher representations for distillation but harms student training when combined with soft-label objectives.

\subsection{Deformable and dynamic convolutions}
\label{sec:related:deformable}

Deformable Convolutional Networks (DCN) \citep{dai2017deformable} introduced learnable offsets for adaptive sampling.
Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} constrains offsets along a one-dimensional path, forcing snake-like sampling that traces elongated structures.

Our DSCFormer integrates DSConv as a \emph{parallel} branch alongside SegFormer-B2, preserving global context while adding crack-specific geometric bias.

\subsection{Knowledge distillation for segmentation}
\label{sec:related:kd}

Knowledge distillation (KD) \citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a smaller student by matching softened output distributions.
In semantic segmentation, structured KD approaches \citep{liu2019structured,wang2020intra} distil spatial relationships rather than just per-pixel logits.
Multi-teacher distillation \citep{you2017learning,yuan2021multihead} combines knowledge from multiple specialised teachers, but typically uses uniform teacher weighting across all classes.

Our class-conditional dual-teacher strategy differs by assigning per-class teacher weights based on each teacher's domain-specific strengths: the foundation-model teacher (SAM-LoRA) receives higher weight for crack classes where its pretrained representations capture global connectivity, while the task-specific teacher (DSCFormer) receives higher weight for spalling where domain-specific features are more effective.

\subsection{Foundation models for segmentation}
\label{sec:related:foundation}

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} represent powerful vision foundation models.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} enables adaptation with minimal trainable parameters (${\sim}5\%$).
While promising on natural images, their effectiveness on small-scale industrial datasets remains underexplored.
We show that rather than directly deploying foundation models, using them as teachers in knowledge distillation yields better results.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the DSCFormer framework. Our approach enhances a SegFormer-B2 backbone at two complementary levels: (1)~\emph{feature extraction} via a parallel DSConv branch for geometry-aware crack sampling, and (2)~\emph{knowledge transfer} via class-conditional dual-teacher distillation (DTKD) to combine the strengths of task-specific and foundation models.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/fig_architecture.png}
    \caption{Overview of the DSCFormer framework. (a)~A SegFormer-B2 encoder with a parallel CrackSnake branch (DSConv) produces 3-class segmentation logits; the DSConv output additively enhances only the crack channel. (b)~Class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD): Teacher~1 (DSCFormer, strong on spalling) and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA, strong on crack connectivity) are combined via per-class weights into an ensemble. A pixel-level agreement map $a(i)$, derived from teacher disagreement, modulates the KD loss so the student learns strongly where teachers agree and falls back on ground-truth supervision at ambiguous pixels.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{SegFormer-B2 backbone}

We adopt SegFormer-B2 \citep{xie2021segformer} as our backbone, providing a hierarchical Mix Transformer (MiT) encoder with four stages at resolutions $H/4$, $H/8$, $H/16$, and $H/32$ with channel dimensions 64, 128, 320, and 512. The all-MLP decoder fuses multi-scale features via linear projection and upsampling. We initialise from weights pretrained on ADE20K at $512{\times}512$.

\subsection{Dynamic Snake Convolution branch}
\label{sec:dsconv}

Standard convolutions sample on fixed rectangular grids---poorly matched to thin, curvilinear cracks. Inspired by \citet{qi2023dynamic}, we introduce a parallel \emph{CrackSnakeBranch} that performs deformable sampling along crack geometry.

\paragraph{Architecture.} The branch consumes encoder stages 1 and 2 (at $H/4$ and $H/8$ resolution). Two parallel DSConv paths deform sampling along the $x$-axis and $y$-axis respectively, each with kernel size $K{=}9$ and hidden dimension $d{=}64$. The outputs are projected to a single-channel crack enhancement map $\mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$.

\paragraph{Additive enhancement.} The map is added \emph{only} to the crack channel logits:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{crack}} = \mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}} + \mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}}
    \label{eq:dsconv}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}}$ denotes the decoder's crack logits. Spalling and background logits remain unmodified.

\paragraph{Zero-initialisation.} The output projection is initialised to zero, so the branch starts as a no-op and gradually learns crack-specific deformations during training.

\subsection{Class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:dtkd}

A key observation from our experiments is that different model architectures excel at different defect types: the task-specific DSCFormer (Teacher~1) achieves higher spalling IoU, while the foundation-model SAM-LoRA (Teacher~2) achieves higher crack connectivity (ConnR). Standard knowledge distillation cannot exploit this complementarity because it applies uniform teacher weighting across all classes.

We propose \emph{class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation} (DTKD) that constructs a per-class ensemble of teacher predictions:

\paragraph{Teacher models.} We use two pretrained teachers, both trained with Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} to enrich their learned representations (the rationale for this choice is validated in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Teacher~1} ($T_1$): DSCFormer (task-specific, strong on spalling)
    \item \textbf{Teacher~2} ($T_2$): SAM-LoRA (foundation model, strong on crack connectivity)
\end{itemize}
We select SAM-LoRA over DINOv2-LoRA as Teacher~2 because SAM-LoRA achieves substantially higher ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ (81.2\% vs.\ 76.5\%) and BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ (68.1\% vs.\ 62.4\%), providing the complementary connectivity knowledge that the task-specific teacher lacks (Table~\ref{tab:main}). DINOv2-LoRA's advantage is limited to IoU$_{\text{sp}}$, which Teacher~1 already covers.

\paragraph{Class-conditional ensemble.} For each class $c$, the ensemble teacher probability is computed as a weighted combination:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{p}_c = w_{1,c} \cdot p_{1,c} + w_{2,c} \cdot p_{2,c}
    \label{eq:dtkd_ensemble}
\end{equation}
where $p_{i,c} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_i / \tau)_c$ is the softened probability from teacher $i$ at temperature $\tau$, and $w_{1,c} + w_{2,c} = 1$ for each class.

Class-conditional weights were determined based on per-class performance analysis of the two teachers on the validation set and fixed before test-set evaluation.
Since Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA) achieves higher ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ while Teacher~1 achieves higher IoU$_{\text{sp}}$, we assign higher Teacher~2 weight for crack and higher Teacher~1 weight for spalling:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Background}: $w_{1,\text{bg}} = w_{2,\text{bg}} = 0.5$ (equal, neither teacher specialises)
    \item \textbf{Crack}: $w_{2,\text{cr}} = 0.6$ (SAM-LoRA has better crack connectivity)
    \item \textbf{Spalling}: $w_{2,\text{sp}} = 0.3$ (DSCFormer has better spalling coverage)
\end{itemize}
The equal-weight baseline (Table~\ref{tab:kd_weights}) shows that this assignment provides modest mIoU gains (+0.1) but notably improves crack centreline preservation (clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.6). Manual weight selection is a limitation; adaptive class-conditional weighting is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\paragraph{Distillation loss.} The ensemble probabilities are renormalised to sum to one and the student is trained with:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = \tau^2 \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{p}} \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_s / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:kd_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ denotes the renormalised class-conditional ensemble probability and $\mathbf{z}_s$ the student logits.

\paragraph{Confidence-aware distillation.}
When two teachers with different architectures and training recipes are combined, their predictions inevitably disagree at certain pixels---particularly at ambiguous crack--background boundaries and thin structures. At such pixels the ensemble soft label is unreliable, and forcing the student to match it may introduce noise.
We quantify per-pixel teacher agreement using the KL divergence between the two softened teacher distributions:
\begin{equation}
    d(i) = \text{KL}\!\left(\text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{T_1}^{(i)} / \tau) \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_{T_2}^{(i)} / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:teacher_disagree}
\end{equation}
where $i$ indexes spatial positions. An agreement map is derived as $a(i) = 1 - d(i) / \max_j d(j)$, ranging from 1 (full agreement) to 0 (maximum disagreement). The confidence-aware distillation loss modulates the per-pixel KD contribution:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}^{\text{conf}} = \tau^2 \cdot \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i} a(i) \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}^{(i)} \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_s^{(i)} / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:conf_kd}
\end{equation}
This ensures the student learns strongly from the ensemble where both teachers concur, but falls back on the ground-truth supervised loss at ambiguous pixels. The agreement map is computed within the existing forward pass (no additional inference cost).
Fig.~\ref{fig:agreement} visualises the disagreement map on representative Hard-tier samples: high-disagreement pixels (bright regions) concentrate at crack--background boundaries and thin branching points, precisely where the ensemble soft label is least reliable.
We empirically validate that teacher disagreement correlates with sample difficulty: the mean per-sample KL divergence between teachers is 43\% higher on Hard-tier images than on Easy-tier images (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_agreement_map.png}
    \caption{Teacher disagreement visualisation on Hard-tier samples. Columns: input, ground truth, Teacher~1 (DSCFormer) prediction, Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA) prediction, pixel-level KL divergence heatmap (bright = high disagreement), student (DTKD) prediction. Disagreement concentrates at crack boundaries and thin structures where the ensemble soft label is unreliable. Red: crack, green: spalling.}

\paragraph{Total loss.} The final training objective balances the segmentation loss and distillation loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha) \left[\lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}\right] + \alpha \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}^{\text{conf}}
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$, and $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 0.5$.
Note that SRL is used only during \emph{teacher} training; the student does not use SRL, as we find it conflicts with the distillation objective (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Test-time augmentation}

At inference, we optionally employ multi-scale TTA: each image is evaluated at three scales (0.75$\times$, 1.0$\times$, 1.25$\times$) with horizontal and vertical flips (9 views). Logits are averaged before argmax.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset}

We evaluate on the \textbf{semantic segmentation subset} of the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, which comprises 3,500 image patches captured during an on-site visit of a concrete dam, supporting classification, detection, and segmentation tasks.
The segmentation subset contains 1,500 pixel-level annotated images with three classes (background, crack, spalling) at $512{\times}512$ resolution, organised into three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard; 500 each) based on crack detection difficulty, enabling curriculum learning.
We split the data into train/val/test sets of 1,200/150/150 images, stratified by tier. Splitting is at the patch level; patches from adjacent regions may share visual characteristics (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset} shows representative samples from each tier with ground-truth overlays; crack pixels range from 0.3\% to 7.9\% and spalling from 1.0\% to 11.7\%, illustrating the extreme class imbalance.

\subsection{Implementation details}

All models are trained on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU using PyTorch.
DSCFormer uses SegFormer-B2 pretrained on ADE20K-512, trained for 100 epochs with AdamW (lr $6{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), 5-epoch warmup, batch size 4.
The DSConv branch uses $d{=}64$, $K{=}9$.
Data augmentation consists of random horizontal and vertical flips ($p{=}0.5$ each); images are resized to $512{\times}512$ and normalised with ImageNet statistics.

For DTKD, Teacher~1 (DSCFormer+SRL) and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA+SRL) are trained independently first with SRL ($\lambda_3 = 0.05$, activated at epoch 60), then frozen during student training.
The student has identical architecture to Teacher~1 but is trained \emph{without} SRL (CE + Dice + KD only).
Distillation uses $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$.

For foundation model baselines, SAM-LoRA uses ViT-B with LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha$=32) and an FPN decoder. DINOv2-LoRA adapts ViT-B/14 similarly with input size $518{\times}518$ (patch-size multiple). Both train for 50 epochs with batch size 2.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

We report complementary metrics capturing different aspects of segmentation quality:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$}: Mean IoU of crack and spalling classes.
    \item \textbf{IoU$_{\text{cr}}$}, \textbf{IoU$_{\text{sp}}$}: Per-class IoU.
    \item \textbf{BF1}: Boundary F1 score (2-pixel tolerance) \citep{csurka2013good}.
    \item \textbf{clDice}: Centreline Dice via skeletonisation \citep{shit2021cldice}.
    \item \textbf{ConnR}: Connectivity Ratio---fraction of GT connected components with $\geq$50\% overlap in prediction.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} summarises the model complexity and inference speed measured on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU at each model's native input resolution.
DSCFormer adds 4.0M parameters (+14.5\%) over SegFormer-B2 due to the DSConv branch, with a modest FPS reduction (124$\to$96).

\paragraph{Benefit of DTKD.}
In the ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, row b$\to$c), adding DTKD to the DSConv-only base improves region-level metrics substantially: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.6, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +2.2, and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.3, at a modest cost to crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ $-$2.6, net ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ $-$0.6).
The resulting ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ (83.0\%) still surpasses SAM-LoRA (81.2\%), confirming that the DSConv geometric prior, partially attenuated by DTKD's soft-label supervision, retains strong connectivity.

\paragraph{Foundation model comparison.}
Despite leveraging powerful pretrained representations, SAM-LoRA (68.1\%) and DINOv2-LoRA (66.7\%) substantially underperform both the base DSCFormer (70.6\%) and DSCFormer+DTKD (72.3\%).
However, our DTKD framework demonstrates that foundation model knowledge can be \emph{indirectly} leveraged: rather than deploying SAM directly, distilling its knowledge into the student yields ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 83.0\% (primarily from DSConv; see ablation), surpassing SAM-LoRA's own 81.2\%.
This represents a more effective use of foundation models on small-scale industrial datasets.

Mask2Former also achieves the highest clDice$_{\text{sp}}$ (91.1\%), but with substantially lower mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ (67.9\%) and IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ (78.6\%), indicating poor spalling coverage despite good boundary and shape capture.

\paragraph{(a)$\to$(b): Adding DSConv.}
The DSConv branch produces minimal mIoU change (+0.1) but substantially improves crack connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ increases by \textbf{+3.7} (81.0$\to$84.7) and clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ by \textbf{+2.8} (82.7$\to$85.5).
The deformable snake-shaped sampling captures elongated crack geometry that fixed rectangular kernels miss, demonstrating that DSConv's primary value lies in its geometric inductive bias for connectivity.

\paragraph{(b)$\to$(c): Adding DTKD.}
DTKD provides substantial pixel-level improvement: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.6, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +2.2, clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.3, and BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.9.
However, ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ decreases from 84.7 to 82.1 ($-$2.6), as DTKD's soft-label supervision slightly smooths fine crack structures; this is partially offset by improved spalling connectivity, yielding a net ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ change of only $-$0.6 (83.6$\to$83.0).
The class-conditional weighting ensures that the student receives the most informative teacher guidance for each defect type (Section~\ref{sec:dtkd}).

\paragraph{(c)$\to$(g): Confidence-aware KD.}
Replacing the standard KD loss with confidence-aware distillation (Eq.~\ref{eq:conf_kd}) improves boundary quality: BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8 (75.6$\to$76.4) and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.6 (83.3$\to$83.9), with a small mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain (+0.2).
This confirms that down-weighting the distillation signal at high-disagreement pixels reduces noise from unreliable ensemble labels.
ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ decreases modestly (82.1$\to$81.4), as the reduced KD weight at ambiguous boundary pixels allows the student to rely more on ground truth---which improves pixel precision but slightly reduces connectivity.

To isolate the contribution of confidence-aware KD from difficulty-guided sample reweighting (an alternative use of teacher disagreement), we conducted an ablation using disagreement-based per-sample loss reweighting \emph{without} pixel-level confidence modulation.
This variant degraded performance (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 72.1, BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ 70.0), indicating that on small datasets, per-sample reweighting introduces training instability, while pixel-level modulation of the distillation signal is the effective mechanism.

Analysis of teacher disagreement across difficulty tiers reveals that the mean per-sample KL divergence between teachers is 0.046 on Hard-tier images vs.\ 0.032 on Easy-tier images (+43\%), confirming that disagreement is an informative proxy for sample difficulty.
However, this correlation does not translate into effective sample-level curriculum learning---consistent with prior findings that data-restricting or reweighting curriculum strategies underperform on small datasets where every sample matters.

\paragraph{(c)$\to$(d): Test-time augmentation.}
TTA provides modest additional gains (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.4, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8) but reduces connectivity metrics (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 83.0$\to$80.2), suggesting that multi-scale/flip aggregation improves pixel coverage but may smooth fine structural details.

\paragraph{Effect of SRL (rows e--f).}
\label{sec:srl}
Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} penalises low predicted probability at ground-truth skeleton pixels $\mathcal{S}$, precomputed offline via morphological skeletonisation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SRL}} = 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{S}} \hat{y}_{\text{crack}}(p)
    \label{eq:srl}
\end{equation}
We apply SRL only to the crack class (activated at epoch 60, weight $\lambda_3 = 0.05$).
Rows (e)--(f) show the effect of adding SRL to the pipeline.
Adding SRL to DSConv (row e) provides small crack improvements (IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.4, BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.0) but severely damages connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ drops from 83.6 to 77.8 ($-$5.8), driven by ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$ falling from 82.4 to 73.4 as SRL's crack-focused supervision fragments spalling components at ambiguous boundaries.
Adding DTKD on top of SRL (row f) partially recovers connectivity (77.8$\to$80.6) but the resulting model (f, mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 71.2\%) underperforms the SRL-free pipeline (c, mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 72.3\%) by \textbf{$-$1.1}.
This demonstrates that SRL's gradient interference with DTKD's soft-label supervision is a net negative: the student learns better from teacher signals alone without the competing skeleton objective.
Multi-seed experiments (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}) further confirm that the SRL path is unstable (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ std 2.42 vs.\ 0.52 for DTKD alone).

\paragraph{Overall progression.}
From baseline (a) to the full method with confidence-aware KD (g), the improvements are: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +2.2, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.8, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +2.6, BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ +2.5, clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +3.4, and ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +5.6.
The three components are complementary: DSConv provides the geometric inductive bias for connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6), DTKD adds pixel-level accuracy across all metrics at a modest cost to crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ $-$2.6, net ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ $-$0.6), and confidence-aware KD further refines boundary quality (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8) by suppressing noisy distillation at ambiguous pixels.
DTKD also acts as a strong regulariser: multi-seed experiments (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}) show it reduces cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3$\times$ (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ std: 0.52 vs.\ 1.47 for the baseline), confirming that distillation is the critical component for both performance and reliability.

\paragraph{Statistical significance.}
To verify that DTKD's gains are not due to random variation, we conduct paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the test images, comparing DSConv-only (row~b) vs.\ DSConv+DTKD (row~c, final model).
Six of ten per-class metrics reach significance at $\alpha{=}0.05$: IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.008$), Dice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.007$), BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.046$), clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{<}0.001$), IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ ($p{=}0.016$), and Dice$_{\text{sp}}$ ($p{=}0.016$).
The four non-significant metrics (BF1$_{\text{sp}}$, clDice$_{\text{sp}}$, ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$, ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$) all involve small paired-sample sizes ($n{=}31$--37) and show directionally consistent improvements, except ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ where DSConv-only is marginally better ($-$1.7, $p{=}0.040$).
Tier-stratified analysis reveals the significance concentrates on Hard images: IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.003$), Dice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.004$), BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.045$), and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{<}0.001$) all reach significance, confirming that the foundation-model teacher's global knowledge provides the strongest benefit on complex crack patterns.

\paragraph{Teacher selection ablation.}
Since the DTKD student does not use SRL, one might expect a teacher trained \emph{without} SRL (higher ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$) to provide cleaner knowledge transfer.
We test this by replacing Teacher~1 (DSCFormer+SRL, ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 77.8\%) with the SRL-free DSConv-only variant (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 83.6\%).
The resulting student achieves 71.1\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, \textbf{$-$1.2} below the G1 teacher variant (72.3\%).
The degradation concentrates on spalling (IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$2.4, ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$ $-$1.4), while crack metrics are largely unaffected.
This indicates that SRL-trained Teacher~1 learns richer internal representations---despite its own connectivity regression---that transfer more effectively through soft-label distillation.
Teacher quality for KD is not simply a function of the teacher's own test metrics; the diversity of learned features matters more.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{DTKD as the critical component.}
A key finding is that DTKD alone---without SRL---provides the strongest and most reliable improvement.
Adding DTKD to DSConv yields +1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ and +2.4 IoU$_{\text{sp}}$, though at a modest cost to crack connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ drops from 84.7 to 82.1 ($-$2.6), as the soft-label supervision slightly smooths fine crack structures. This is partially offset by improved spalling connectivity, yielding a net ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ change of only $-$0.6 (83.6$\to$83.0). The trade-off reflects that pixel-level and topological objectives are not perfectly aligned; practitioners prioritising crack path continuity over overall pixel accuracy may prefer the DSConv-only variant.
In contrast, the SRL+DTKD path (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, row f) achieves only 71.2\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$---1.1 points lower---because SRL's skeleton-level gradients conflict with DTKD's soft-label supervision.
The class-conditional weighting steers the distillation signal toward better crack centreline preservation (clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.6 over equal weighting) by assigning higher SAM-LoRA weight for crack (0.6) and higher DSCFormer weight for spalling (0.7), though the overall mIoU difference is marginal (+0.1).
Beyond metric improvement, DTKD serves as a powerful training regulariser: multi-seed experiments (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}) show that DTKD reduces cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3--5$\times$ (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ std: 0.52 vs.\ 1.47 for the baseline, vs.\ 2.42 for DSCFormer+SRL).
The soft-label supervision from two pretrained teachers smooths the loss landscape, making convergence less sensitive to random initialisation---a practical advantage for deployment scenarios where retraining reliability matters.

\paragraph{Teacher disagreement as a quality signal.}
An advantage of multi-teacher distillation is that the \emph{disagreement} between teachers provides a free quality signal.
We find that per-pixel KL divergence between teachers is 43\% higher on Hard-tier images than Easy-tier images, confirming that disagreement correlates with sample difficulty.
Leveraging this signal at the pixel level (confidence-aware KD) is effective: down-weighting distillation at high-disagreement pixels improves BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ by +0.8 and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ by +0.6.
However, using the same signal for \emph{sample-level} curriculum learning (reweighting entire samples by their aggregate disagreement) degrades performance, consistent with prior findings that curriculum strategies that alter per-sample training intensity are counterproductive on small datasets where every sample is needed for generalisation.
This distinction---pixel-level modulation helps, sample-level modulation hurts---suggests that the granularity of the intervention matters more than the quality of the difficulty signal.

\paragraph{SRL: useful for teachers, harmful for students.}
An intriguing finding is that SRL plays opposite roles depending on where it is applied.
As a \emph{teacher training} component, SRL is beneficial: Teacher~1 (trained with SRL) produces a better DTKD student (72.3\%) than a teacher trained without SRL (71.1\%), despite the SRL teacher's own lower ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ (77.8\% vs.\ 83.6\%).
This suggests SRL forces the teacher to learn richer internal representations that transfer well through distillation.
However, as a \emph{student training} component, SRL is harmful: it interferes with DTKD's soft-label supervision, fragmenting spalling predictions and increasing training instability.
This asymmetry highlights that the optimal training recipe for a teacher may differ from that of a distillation student.

\paragraph{Indirect use of foundation models.}
Our results reveal a practical strategy for leveraging foundation models on small industrial datasets: rather than directly fine-tuning SAM or DINOv2 (which underperforms task-specific architectures by 2--4\% mIoU), using them as \emph{teachers} in knowledge distillation allows the student to inherit their structural knowledge while maintaining the task-specific model's advantages.
This is more parameter-efficient at inference time (student has identical cost to the base model) and more effective than direct deployment.

\paragraph{DSConv mechanism.}
The DSConv branch's effectiveness stems from its geometric inductive bias: by constraining kernel deformations to follow elongated paths, it captures crack features that rectangular convolutions systematically miss.
The zero-initialisation ensures the branch only activates when beneficial.
The ConnR improvement (+6.6 in ablation) confirms that DSConv preserves crack connectivity at the connected-component level.

\paragraph{Practical implications.}
From an inspection workflow perspective, DSCFormer+DTKD offers a practical balance: the student model has identical inference cost to the base DSCFormer (96 FPS, 31.3M parameters), with the two teachers used only during training.
The per-tier analysis suggests prioritising model deployment on Hard-tier regions where DTKD provides the largest benefit, while Easy regions may not require topology-aware enhancements.
For integration with existing dam SHM pipelines, the model's crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 83.0\%, driven primarily by DSConv) helps produce predictions that are directly usable for crack length and density estimation without requiring extensive post-processing to reconnect fragmented segments.

\paragraph{Failure modes.}
The model underperforms in two scenarios: (i)~thin hairline cracks (${<}$2 pixels wide) where even DSConv's deformable sampling cannot capture sufficient signal, and (ii)~regions where crack and spalling co-occur, where the model tends to over-predict one class at the expense of the other.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The DamSegment dataset comprises images from a single dam site, and the train/val/test split is at the patch level; spatially adjacent patches may share visual characteristics, potentially inflating in-domain metrics. Scene-level or location-based splitting would provide a more conservative evaluation. The S2DS cross-dataset transfer partially mitigates this concern but reveals a substantial domain gap (${\sim}$19\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), indicating that in-domain results should not be directly extrapolated to other sites. Multi-site datasets would strengthen generalisability claims.
The per-class teacher weights in DTKD are set manually based on validation-set performance analysis; adaptive class-conditional weighting is an avenue for future work.
Crack IoU remains challenging (${\sim}$59\%) due to extreme class imbalance (crack pixels ${<}$5\% of total area).
The DSConv branch is crack-specific; extending to other elongated defects is future work.
Cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS demonstrates improved relative robustness of DSConv-equipped models, but the absolute mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ remains low (${\sim}$19\%), indicating that zero-shot transfer across concrete inspection domains is far from solved; domain adaptation or mixed-dataset training may be necessary for practical cross-site deployment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented DSCFormer, a topology-aware segmentation framework for concrete dam defect analysis incorporating two complementary enhancements to a SegFormer-B2 backbone.
Dynamic Snake Convolution drives crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6) with only 4M additional parameters, and dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD) drives pixel-level accuracy (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9 over DSConv alone) at zero additional inference cost, while reducing cross-seed variance by ${\sim}$3--5$\times$. Confidence-aware distillation, which modulates KD intensity based on pixel-level teacher agreement, further improves boundary quality (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.8, clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.6). DTKD trades modest crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ $-$2.6) for substantial pixel-level gains; the net ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ ($-$0.6) reflects improved spalling connectivity partially offsetting the crack regression.
We also investigated Skeleton Recall Loss and found an asymmetric effect: SRL benefits teacher training (producing richer representations for distillation) but harms student training when combined with DTKD ($-$1.1 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), highlighting that optimal training recipes differ between teachers and distillation students.
The full framework achieves 72.3\% foreground mIoU (72.7\% with TTA), the highest among all compared methods on the DamSegment benchmark. ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ (83.0\%) primarily reflects DSConv's contribution; the DSConv-only variant achieves a slightly higher 83.6\%.
Class-conditional teacher weighting modestly improves crack centreline preservation (clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.6) over equal weighting, serving as a practical design choice within DTKD rather than a core contribution.
Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS confirms that DSConv's geometric inductive bias substantially improves cross-domain robustness (18.7\% vs.\ 0.1\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), though absolute performance remains limited, motivating future work on domain adaptation.
Our work demonstrates that indirectly leveraging foundation models through knowledge distillation is more effective than direct parameter-efficient fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets, providing practical guidance for deploying deep learning in data-scarce inspection scenarios.
