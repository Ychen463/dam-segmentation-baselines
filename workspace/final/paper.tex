\begin{abstract}
Automated semantic segmentation of cracks and spalling on concrete dam surfaces is essential for structural health monitoring, yet existing methods optimise pixel-level metrics without explicitly preserving the structural continuity critical for safety assessment.
We propose \textbf{DSCFormer}, a topology-aware segmentation framework incorporating three complementary enhancements to a SegFormer-B2 backbone:
(1)~a \emph{Dynamic Snake Convolution} (DSConv) branch that captures elongated crack geometry through deformable sampling, adding only 4M parameters (+14.5\%);
(2)~\emph{Skeleton Recall Loss} (SRL) that penalises predictions missing ground-truth centreline pixels, improving skeleton-level recall with negligible runtime overhead; and
(3)~\emph{Class-Conditional Dual-Teacher Knowledge Distillation} (DTKD) that transfers complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher with per-class weighting, \emph{at zero additional inference cost}.
On the \textbf{DamSegment} dataset \citep{damsegment2026} (1,500 images, 3~classes), DSCFormer+DTKD achieves 71.2\% foreground mIoU (72.2\% with TTA), the highest among all compared methods, with 86.2\% centreline Dice and 80.6\% connectivity ratio.
Ablation experiments confirm complementary gains: DSConv improves crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6), SRL adds crack boundary accuracy (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.0), and DTKD boosts all metrics (mIoU +0.6, ConnR +2.8), with improvements concentrated on Hard-tier images (+2.5 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$).
Class-conditional teacher weighting outperforms equal weighting by +1.2 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, validating the per-class specialisation strategy.
Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS demonstrates that DSConv-equipped models achieve 17.1\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, compared to 0.1\% for SegFormer-B2 and 2.5\% for Mask2Former, indicating that the geometric inductive bias substantially improves cross-domain robustness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Concrete dams are safety-critical infrastructure whose structural integrity must be continuously monitored throughout multi-decade service lives.
Surface defects---predominantly cracks and spalling---serve as early indicators of internal deterioration, yet their manual inspection remains labour-intensive, subjective, and logistically constrained by the scale and accessibility of dam faces \citep{chen2024damsurvey,fang2024dam}.
Automated semantic segmentation of these defects from high-resolution imagery therefore promises substantial gains in inspection efficiency and repeatability.

Recent deep-learning methods have advanced pixel-level defect segmentation considerably.
Encoder--decoder architectures such as U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} and hierarchical transformers like SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} achieve competitive Intersection-over-Union (IoU) on benchmark crack datasets \citep{zou2019deepcrack,liu2019deepcrack}.
However, IoU and related region-overlap metrics are insensitive to topological errors: a prediction that fragments a continuous crack into disconnected segments may still score favourably on IoU while misrepresenting the structural extent of the defect \citep{pantoja2024cracktopology}.
For structural safety assessment, the \emph{connectivity} of a predicted crack is as important as its pixel coverage---a fragmented prediction can lead an engineer to underestimate propagation length and thus misjudge residual capacity.

Topology-preserving losses such as clDice \citep{shit2021cldice} and persistent-homology penalties \citep{hu2019topology,clough2020topological} partially address this gap, yet they either incur prohibitive GPU memory for high-resolution dam images or operate on binary masks without multi-class support.
The recently proposed Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{shit2024skeleton} overcomes both limitations by computing recall on CPU-extracted ground-truth skeletons with over 90\% reduction in computational overhead.
Concurrently, Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} reshapes kernel sampling grids to follow elongated, tortuous paths---a morphological prior that aligns naturally with the geometry of cracks.

Meanwhile, large vision foundation models such as SAM \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} offer powerful pretrained representations, yet our experiments show that parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} on small industrial datasets underperforms fully fine-tuned task-specific architectures.
A natural question arises: \emph{can the complementary strengths of task-specific and foundation models be combined}?
We address this through knowledge distillation, where a student model learns from both a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher with class-conditional weighting.

In this paper we propose \textbf{DSCFormer}, a framework that augments SegFormer-B2 with a parallel DSConv branch, SRL training, and class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD).
We evaluate on the semantic segmentation subset of the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, comprising 1,500 annotated dam surface images.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A topology-aware segmentation framework combining Dynamic Snake Convolution with Skeleton Recall Loss for fine-grained dam defect segmentation, where DSConv improves crack connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6) and SRL adds crack boundary accuracy (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.0), with only 4M additional parameters and no inference-time overhead from distillation.
    \item A class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation strategy (DTKD) that transfers complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher (strong on spalling) and a foundation-model teacher (strong on crack connectivity) with per-class weighting, improving mIoU by +0.6 and ConnR by +2.8 over the base model. An ablation study confirms that class-conditional weighting outperforms equal weighting by +1.2 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$.
    \item A comprehensive evaluation including comparison with six baselines (including SAM-LoRA and DINOv2-LoRA), per-tier difficulty analysis, statistical significance testing, and zero-shot cross-dataset transfer on S2DS, demonstrating that task-specific design with knowledge distillation outperforms direct foundation-model fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets and that DSConv's geometric inductive bias substantially improves cross-domain robustness.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Concrete defect segmentation}
\label{sec:related:defect}

Early CNN-based crack detectors such as CrackNet treated defect identification as patch classification \citep{zou2019deepcrack}.
Fully convolutional designs subsequently enabled dense pixel-wise prediction: DeepCrack \citep{liu2019deepcrack} introduced deeply-supervised side outputs, while \citet{zou2019deepcrack} exploited hierarchical features with multi-scale fusion.

Transformer-based architectures have demonstrated improved global context modelling.
CrackFormer \citep{liu2021crackformer} embeds self-attention to capture long-range crack continuity, and SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} provides a lightweight hierarchical encoder with an all-MLP decoder.
Universal architectures such as Mask2Former \citep{cheng2022mask2former} further push segmentation accuracy on general benchmarks but have seen limited adoption for infrastructure defects due to annotation scarcity.

Applied to dam inspection specifically, \citet{arafin2024concrete} performed multi-class concrete defect segmentation using DeepLabV3+, while \citet{fang2024dam} proposed a lightweight architecture for microscopic dam crack quantification.
\citet{chen2024damsurvey} surveyed visual perception methods for intelligent dam inspection, identifying limited dataset scale and poor topology preservation as key open challenges.

\subsection{Topology-aware segmentation}
\label{sec:related:topology}

\citet{hu2019topology} introduced a differentiable topological loss based on persistent homology.
\citet{clough2020topological} extended persistent-homology supervision to cardiac imaging.
However, persistent-homology computation scales poorly to high-resolution images.

For tubular structures, \citet{shit2021cldice} proposed clDice, a differentiable soft-skeleton metric, but it requires GPU-based skeletonisation with substantial memory overhead.
The Skeleton Recall Loss \citep{shit2024skeleton} pre-computes ground-truth skeletons on CPU and formulates a recall objective, achieving superior connectivity with over 90\% memory reduction relative to clDice.

Our work extends SRL to the multi-class dam defect setting and pairs it with geometry-aware convolutions and knowledge distillation.

\subsection{Deformable and dynamic convolutions}
\label{sec:related:deformable}

Deformable Convolutional Networks (DCN) \citep{dai2017deformable} introduced learnable offsets for adaptive sampling.
Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} constrains offsets along a one-dimensional path, forcing snake-like sampling that traces elongated structures.

Our DSCFormer integrates DSConv as a \emph{parallel} branch alongside SegFormer-B2, preserving global context while adding crack-specific geometric bias.

\subsection{Knowledge distillation for segmentation}
\label{sec:related:kd}

Knowledge distillation (KD) \citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a smaller student by matching softened output distributions.
In semantic segmentation, structured KD approaches \citep{liu2019structured,wang2020intra} distil spatial relationships rather than just per-pixel logits.
Multi-teacher distillation \citep{you2017learning,yuan2021multihead} combines knowledge from multiple specialised teachers, but typically uses uniform teacher weighting across all classes.

Our class-conditional dual-teacher strategy differs by assigning per-class teacher weights based on each teacher's domain-specific strengths: the foundation-model teacher (SAM-LoRA) receives higher weight for crack classes where its pretrained representations capture global connectivity, while the task-specific teacher (DSCFormer) receives higher weight for spalling where domain-specific features are more effective.

\subsection{Foundation models for segmentation}
\label{sec:related:foundation}

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} represent powerful vision foundation models.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} enables adaptation with minimal trainable parameters (${\sim}5\%$).
While promising on natural images, their effectiveness on small-scale industrial datasets remains underexplored.
We show that rather than directly deploying foundation models, using them as teachers in knowledge distillation yields better results.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the DSCFormer framework. Our approach enhances a SegFormer-B2 backbone at three complementary levels: (1)~\emph{feature extraction} via a parallel DSConv branch for geometry-aware crack sampling, (2)~\emph{training objective} via SRL for explicit skeleton supervision, and (3)~\emph{knowledge transfer} via class-conditional dual-teacher distillation to combine the strengths of task-specific and foundation models.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.90\textwidth]{figures/fig_architecture.png}
    \caption{Overview of the DSCFormer framework. (a)~A SegFormer-B2 encoder with a parallel DSConv branch produces segmentation logits. (b)~Training uses a composite loss (CE + Dice + SRL) combined with class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation, where Teacher~1 (DSCFormer) guides spalling predictions and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA) guides crack predictions.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{SegFormer-B2 backbone}

We adopt SegFormer-B2 \citep{xie2021segformer} as our backbone, providing a hierarchical Mix Transformer (MiT) encoder with four stages at resolutions $H/4$, $H/8$, $H/16$, and $H/32$ with channel dimensions 64, 128, 320, and 512. The all-MLP decoder fuses multi-scale features via linear projection and upsampling. We initialise from weights pretrained on ADE20K at $512{\times}512$.

\subsection{Dynamic Snake Convolution branch}
\label{sec:dsconv}

Standard convolutions sample on fixed rectangular grids---poorly matched to thin, curvilinear cracks. Inspired by \citet{qi2023dynamic}, we introduce a parallel \emph{CrackSnakeBranch} that performs deformable sampling along crack geometry.

\paragraph{Architecture.} The branch consumes encoder stages 1 and 2 (at $H/4$ and $H/8$ resolution). Two parallel DSConv paths deform sampling along the $x$-axis and $y$-axis respectively, each with kernel size $K{=}9$ and hidden dimension $d{=}64$. The outputs are projected to a single-channel crack enhancement map $\mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$.

\paragraph{Additive enhancement.} The map is added \emph{only} to the crack channel logits:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{crack}} = \mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}} + \mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}}
    \label{eq:dsconv}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}}$ denotes the decoder's crack logits. Spalling and background logits remain unmodified.

\paragraph{Zero-initialisation.} The output projection is initialised to zero, so the branch starts as a no-op and gradually learns crack-specific deformations during training.

\subsection{Skeleton Recall Loss}
\label{sec:srl}

Let $\mathcal{S} = \{p_1, \ldots, p_{|\mathcal{S}|}\}$ denote the ground-truth skeleton pixels for the crack class, precomputed offline via morphological skeletonisation. SRL \citep{shit2024skeleton} penalises low predicted probability at centreline locations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SRL}} = 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{S}} \hat{y}_{\text{crack}}(p)
    \label{eq:srl}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}_{\text{crack}}(p) \in [0,1]$ is the softmax probability for the crack class at pixel $p$.

Unlike clDice \citep{shit2021cldice} which requires GPU-based differentiable skeletonisation, SRL operates on precomputed skeletons with no runtime cost. We activate SRL only after epoch 60 with weight $\lambda_3 = 0.05$ to avoid gradient instability during early training.

\subsection{Class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:dtkd}

A key observation from our experiments is that different model architectures excel at different defect types: the task-specific DSCFormer (Teacher~1) achieves higher spalling IoU, while the foundation-model SAM-LoRA (Teacher~2) achieves higher crack connectivity (ConnR). Standard knowledge distillation cannot exploit this complementarity because it applies uniform teacher weighting across all classes.

We propose \emph{class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation} (DTKD) that constructs a per-class ensemble of teacher predictions:

\paragraph{Teacher models.} We use two pretrained teachers:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Teacher~1} ($T_1$): DSCFormer trained with SRL (task-specific, strong on spalling)
    \item \textbf{Teacher~2} ($T_2$): SAM-LoRA trained with SRL (foundation model, strong on crack connectivity)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Class-conditional ensemble.} For each class $c$, the ensemble teacher probability is computed as a weighted combination:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{p}_c = w_{1,c} \cdot p_{1,c} + w_{2,c} \cdot p_{2,c}
    \label{eq:dtkd_ensemble}
\end{equation}
where $p_{i,c} = \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_i / \tau)_c$ is the softened probability from teacher $i$ at temperature $\tau$, and $w_{1,c} + w_{2,c} = 1$ for each class.

Class-conditional weights were determined based on per-class performance analysis of the two teachers on the validation set and fixed before test-set evaluation:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Background}: $w_{1,\text{bg}} = w_{2,\text{bg}} = 0.5$ (equal)
    \item \textbf{Crack}: $w_{2,\text{cr}} = 0.6$ (SAM-LoRA has better crack connectivity)
    \item \textbf{Spalling}: $w_{2,\text{sp}} = 0.3$ (DSCFormer has better spalling coverage)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Distillation loss.} The ensemble probabilities are renormalised to sum to one and the student is trained with:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = \tau^2 \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{p}} \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_s / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:kd_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}$ denotes the renormalised class-conditional ensemble probability and $\mathbf{z}_s$ the student logits.

\paragraph{Total loss.} The final training objective balances the segmentation loss and distillation loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha) \left[\lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}} + \lambda_3(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{SRL}}\right] + \alpha \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$, $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 0.5$, and $\lambda_3(t) = 0.05$ for $t \geq 60$.

\subsection{Test-time augmentation}

At inference, we optionally employ multi-scale TTA: each image is evaluated at three scales (0.75$\times$, 1.0$\times$, 1.25$\times$) with horizontal and vertical flips (9 views). Logits are averaged before argmax.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset}

We evaluate on the \textbf{semantic segmentation subset} of the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, comprising 1,500 pixel-level annotated images of concrete dam surfaces from U.S.\ dams.
The dataset contains three classes (background, crack, spalling) at $512{\times}512$ resolution, organised into three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard; 500 each).
We split the data into train/val/test sets of 1,200/150/150 images, stratified by tier.

\subsection{Implementation details}

All models are trained on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU using PyTorch.
DSCFormer uses SegFormer-B2 pretrained on ADE20K-512, trained for 100 epochs with AdamW (lr $6{\times}10^{-5}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), 5-epoch warmup, batch size 4.
The DSConv branch uses $d{=}64$, $K{=}9$.
SRL activates at epoch 60 with $\lambda_3 = 0.05$.

For DTKD, Teacher~1 (DSCFormer+SRL) and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA+SRL) are trained independently first, then frozen during student training.
The student has identical architecture to Teacher~1.
Distillation uses $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$.

For foundation model baselines, SAM-LoRA uses ViT-B with LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha$=32) and an FPN decoder. DINOv2-LoRA adapts ViT-B/14 similarly with input size $518{\times}518$ (patch-size multiple). Both train for 50 epochs with batch size 2.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

We report complementary metrics capturing different aspects of segmentation quality:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$}: Mean IoU of crack and spalling classes.
    \item \textbf{IoU$_{\text{cr}}$}, \textbf{IoU$_{\text{sp}}$}: Per-class IoU.
    \item \textbf{BF1}: Boundary F1 score (2-pixel tolerance) \citep{csurka2013good}.
    \item \textbf{clDice}: Centreline Dice via skeletonisation \citep{shit2021cldice}.
    \item \textbf{ConnR}: Connectivity Ratio---fraction of GT connected components with $\geq$50\% overlap in prediction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Computational cost}

Table~\ref{tab:cost} summarises the model complexity and inference speed measured on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU at each model's native input resolution.
DSCFormer adds 4.0M parameters (+14.5\%) over SegFormer-B2 due to the DSConv branch, with a modest FPS reduction (124$\to$96).
Crucially, DTKD incurs \emph{no additional inference cost}: the two teachers are only used during training, and the student has identical architecture to the base DSCFormer.

\paragraph{Benefit of DTKD.}
Adding DTKD to the base DSCFormer improves all metrics simultaneously: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.6, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.7, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +0.7, BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.1, clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.7, and ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ \textbf{+2.8}.
The ConnR improvement is particularly notable: DTKD recovers the connectivity loss introduced by SRL (see Section~\ref{sec:ablation}), raising ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ from 77.8\% to 80.6\%.
This confirms that the foundation-model teacher's global structural knowledge complements the task-specific model's local feature quality.

\paragraph{Foundation model comparison.}
Despite leveraging powerful pretrained representations, SAM-LoRA (68.1\%) and DINOv2-LoRA (66.7\%) substantially underperform both the base DSCFormer (70.6\%) and DSCFormer+DTKD (71.2\%).
However, our DTKD framework demonstrates that foundation model knowledge can be \emph{indirectly} leveraged: rather than deploying SAM directly, distilling its crack connectivity knowledge into the student improves ConnR by +2.8 over the base model.
This represents a more effective use of foundation models on small-scale industrial datasets.

Mask2Former also achieves the highest clDice$_{\text{sp}}$ (91.1\%), but with substantially lower mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ (67.9\%) and IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ (78.6\%), indicating poor spalling coverage despite good boundary and shape capture.

The DSConv branch produces minimal mIoU change (+0.1) but substantially improves crack connectivity: ConnR$_{\text{cr}}$ increases by \textbf{+3.7} (81.0$\to$84.7) and clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ by \textbf{+2.8} (82.7$\to$85.5).
The deformable snake-shaped sampling captures elongated crack geometry that fixed rectangular kernels miss, demonstrating that DSConv's primary value lies in its geometric inductive bias for connectivity.

\paragraph{(b)$\to$(c): Adding SRL.}
SRL improves crack IoU (+0.4), BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ (+1.0), and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ (+0.5) by focusing gradient signal on centreline pixels.
However, ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ decreases from 83.6 to 77.8, driven by ConnR$_{\text{sp}}$ dropping from 82.4 to 73.4.
This occurs because SRL's crack-focused supervision encourages the model to predict more crack pixels at ambiguous crack-spalling boundaries, fragmenting spalling components.

\paragraph{(c)$\to$(d): Adding DTKD.}
DTKD provides the most balanced improvement: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.6, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.7, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +0.7, and ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ \textbf{+2.8} (77.8$\to$80.6).
Crucially, DTKD partially recovers the ConnR loss introduced by SRL, because the foundation-model teacher provides global structural knowledge that helps maintain connected-component integrity.
The class-conditional weighting ensures that the student receives the most informative guidance for each defect type.

\paragraph{(d)$\to$(e): Test-time augmentation.}
TTA improves region-overlap metrics (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.0, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +0.9, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +0.9) but slightly reduces some topology-sensitive metrics (BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ 71.4$\to$71.1, clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ 86.2$\to$84.6, ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ 80.6$\to$79.7).
This suggests that multi-scale/flip aggregation improves pixel coverage but may smooth fine boundary structures.

\paragraph{Overall progression.}
From baseline (a) to full method (d), the improvements are: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +0.9, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.1, BF1$_{\text{fg}}$ +2.8, clDice$_{\text{fg}}$ +3.5, ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +3.6.
The three components are complementary: DSConv improves \emph{feature representation} (ConnR, clDice), SRL improves the \emph{training signal} (IoU, BF1), and DTKD improves \emph{knowledge transfer} from complementary models (all metrics).

\paragraph{Statistical significance.}
To verify that DTKD's gains over DSCFormer are not due to random variation, we conduct paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the 150 test images.
DTKD significantly improves crack segmentation: IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.011$), BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{=}0.006$), and clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ ($p{<}0.001$), with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals excluding zero.
The improvement is most pronounced on Hard-tier images (clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ $p{<}0.001$, IoU$_{\text{cr}}$ $p{=}0.020$), where complex crack patterns benefit most from the foundation-model teacher's global knowledge.
Spalling improvements are directionally consistent but not statistically significant at $\alpha{=}0.05$ ($p{=}0.135$ for IoU$_{\text{sp}}$), likely due to the smaller number of spalling-containing test images.

DTKD's advantage is most pronounced on Hard-tier images, where mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ improves by +2.5 over SegFormer-B2 (66.9 vs.\ 64.4) and IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ by +3.9 (79.7 vs.\ 75.8), compared to near-parity on Easy.

\subsection{Cross-dataset evaluation}
\label{sec:cross_dataset}

To assess generalisability beyond the training domain, we perform zero-shot transfer evaluation on the S2DS dataset \citep{s2ds2021}, which contains 743 images ($1024{\times}1024$) of concrete surfaces with pixel-level annotations for seven defect classes: background, crack, spalling, corrosion, efflorescence, vegetation, and control point.
We map S2DS crack and spalling to our label scheme and treat all other classes as background, since they fall outside the DamSegment label space.
All images are resized to $512{\times}512$ (bilinear for images, nearest-neighbour for masks).
The S2DS test split contains 93~images, of which 27 contain only crack, 20 contain only spalling, and 46 are background-only; crack pixels account for 0.12\% and spalling for 4.27\% of the test area.
No fine-tuning is performed: models trained on DamSegment are applied directly.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Complementary role of DTKD.}
A key finding is that DTKD addresses the ConnR trade-off introduced by SRL.
While SRL improves crack metrics at the cost of spalling connectivity (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$: 83.6$\to$77.8), DTKD partially recovers this (77.8$\to$80.6) by injecting global structural knowledge from the foundation-model teacher.
The per-tier analysis (Table~\ref{tab:per_tier}) further validates this: on Medium-tier images, ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ recovers from 68.9 (DSCFormer+SRL) to 76.9 (DTKD), and the overall mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ advantage of DTKD grows from near-zero on Easy to +2.5 on Hard, confirming that the foundation-model teacher is most beneficial for complex defect patterns.
The class-conditional weighting is essential: the SAM-based teacher receives higher weight for crack (0.6), where its pretrained representations capture global connectivity, while the task-specific teacher dominates spalling predictions (weight 0.7), where domain-specific features are more effective.

\paragraph{Indirect use of foundation models.}
Our results reveal a practical strategy for leveraging foundation models on small industrial datasets: rather than directly fine-tuning SAM or DINOv2 (which underperforms task-specific architectures by 2--4\% mIoU), using them as \emph{teachers} in knowledge distillation allows the student to inherit their structural knowledge while maintaining the task-specific model's advantages.
This is more parameter-efficient at inference time (student has identical cost to the base model) and more effective than direct deployment.

\paragraph{DSConv mechanism.}
The DSConv branch's effectiveness stems from its geometric inductive bias: by constraining kernel deformations to follow elongated paths, it captures crack features that rectangular convolutions systematically miss.
The zero-initialisation ensures the branch only activates when beneficial.
The ConnR improvement (+6.6 in ablation) confirms that DSConv preserves crack connectivity at the connected-component level.

\paragraph{SRL delayed start.}
The delayed-start strategy (epoch 60) is critical: early SRL activation causes training instability as the model attempts topology optimisation before learning basic features.
This staged approach is simpler and more stable than curriculum-based alternatives we explored in preliminary experiments.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The per-class teacher weights in DTKD are currently set manually based on teacher performance analysis; adaptive class-conditional weighting is an avenue for future work.
Crack IoU remains challenging (${\sim}$59\%) due to extreme class imbalance (crack pixels ${<}5\%$ of total area).
The DSConv branch is crack-specific; extending to other elongated defects is future work.
Cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS demonstrates improved relative robustness of DSConv-equipped models, but the absolute mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ remains low (${\sim}$17\%), indicating that zero-shot transfer across concrete inspection domains is far from solved; domain adaptation or mixed-dataset training may be necessary for practical cross-site deployment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented DSCFormer, a topology-aware segmentation framework for concrete dam defect analysis incorporating three complementary enhancements to a SegFormer-B2 backbone.
Dynamic Snake Convolution provides geometry-aware crack feature extraction (ConnR$_{\text{fg}}$ +6.6) with only 4M additional parameters, Skeleton Recall Loss improves crack centreline recall (BF1$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.0), and class-conditional dual-teacher knowledge distillation combines task-specific and foundation-model knowledge (mIoU +0.6, ConnR +2.8) at zero additional inference cost.
The full framework achieves 71.2\% foreground mIoU (72.2\% with TTA), the highest among all compared methods on the DamSegment benchmark, with gains concentrated on Hard-tier images (+2.5 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$) where complex defect patterns benefit most.
Class-conditional teacher weighting proves essential, outperforming equal weighting by +1.2 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$.
Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS confirms that DSConv's geometric inductive bias substantially improves cross-domain robustness (17.1\% vs.\ 0.1\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$), though absolute transfer performance remains limited, motivating future work on domain adaptation.
Our work demonstrates that indirectly leveraging foundation models through knowledge distillation is more effective than direct parameter-efficient fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets, providing practical guidance for deploying deep learning in data-scarce inspection scenarios.
