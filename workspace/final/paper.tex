\begin{abstract}
Automated semantic segmentation of cracks and spalling on concrete dam surfaces is essential for structural health monitoring, yet combining task-specific models with foundation models remains underexplored for infrastructure defects.
We propose \textbf{HeteroDistill}, a framework that augments a SegFormer-B2 backbone with \emph{Heterogeneous Dual-Teacher Knowledge Distillation} (DTKD), which transfers complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher (SAM-LoRA) at zero additional inference cost.
On the \textbf{DamSegment} dataset \citep{damsegment2026} (1{,}500 images, 3~classes), HeteroDistill achieves 72.2--72.3\% foreground mIoU, with DTKD contributing +1.8--1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ and the student surpassing the direct teacher ensemble by +1.5--1.6.
Multi-seed matched controls (5 conditions $\times$ 3 seeds) confirm +1.5 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain with 5--7$\times$ variance reduction, while single-teacher KD provides no reliable benefit---consistent with \emph{teacher diversity} being a key factor.
A parameter-matched branch comparison (DSConv vs.\ DCNv2 vs.\ standard convolution, 3 seeds each) finds no measurable benefit from geometric inductive bias---a negative result relevant to the crack segmentation community.
The resulting lightweight student model (51 FPS, 28.7M parameters) is intended as the vision module in automated dam inspection workflows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Concrete dams are safety-critical infrastructure whose structural integrity must be continuously monitored throughout multi-decade service lives.
Surface defects---predominantly cracks and spalling---serve as early indicators of internal deterioration, yet their manual inspection remains labour-intensive, subjective, and logistically constrained \citep{chen2024damsurvey,li2024dam}.
Automated semantic segmentation of these defects from high-resolution imagery therefore promises substantial gains in inspection efficiency and repeatability.

Recent deep-learning methods have advanced pixel-level defect segmentation considerably.
Encoder--decoder architectures such as U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} and hierarchical transformers like SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} achieve competitive IoU on benchmark crack datasets \citep{zou2019deepcrack,liu2019deepcrack}.
However, IoU is insensitive to topological errors: a prediction that fragments a continuous crack may still score favourably while misrepresenting the structural extent of the defect \citep{pantoja2022topoloss}.
For structural assessment, crack \emph{connectivity} is as important as pixel coverage.

Topology-preserving losses such as clDice \citep{shit2021cldice} and persistent-homology penalties \citep{hu2019topology} partially address this gap, and the Skeleton Recall Loss (SRL) \citep{kirchhoff2024skeleton} reduces computational overhead substantially.
Meanwhile, foundation models such as SAM \citep{kirillov2023sam} offer powerful pretrained representations, yet in our experiments, LoRA fine-tuning \citep{hu2022lora} on small industrial datasets underperforms fully fine-tuned task-specific architectures.
A natural question arises: \emph{can the complementary strengths of task-specific and foundation models be combined}?

We address this through knowledge distillation, proposing \textbf{HeteroDistill}, a framework with dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD) for complementary knowledge transfer.
We evaluate on the DamSegment dataset \citep{damsegment2026}, comprising 1{,}500 annotated dam surface images.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Heterogeneous dual-teacher knowledge distillation} (DTKD) combining a task-specific teacher with a foundation-model teacher at zero inference overhead. Multi-seed matched controls (5 conditions $\times$ 3 seeds) confirm +1.5 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ net gain with 5--7$\times$ variance reduction (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
    \item A \emph{negative result on geometric inductive bias}: parameter-matched comparison of Dynamic Snake Convolution, DCNv2, and standard convolution (3 seeds each, ${\sim}$1.32M parameters) shows no measurable difference (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
    \item Comprehensive evaluation with six baselines, per-tier analysis, multi-seed stability, and zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Concrete defect segmentation}

Early CNN-based crack detectors such as CrackNet \citep{zhang2017cracknet} treated defect identification as pixel-level classification.
Fully convolutional designs subsequently enabled dense prediction: DeepCrack \citep{liu2019deepcrack} introduced deeply-supervised side outputs, while \citet{zou2019deepcrack} exploited hierarchical multi-scale fusion.
Transformer-based architectures have demonstrated improved global context modelling: CrackFormer \citep{liu2021crackformer} embeds self-attention for long-range crack continuity, and SegFormer \citep{xie2021segformer} provides a lightweight hierarchical encoder.
Applied to dam inspection, \citet{arafin2024concrete} performed multi-class concrete defect segmentation using DeepLabV3+, \citet{li2024dam} proposed a lightweight CNN-Transformer hybrid, and CCT~Net \citep{ling2025cctnet} combined CNN and Transformer branches for dam crack segmentation.

\subsection{Topology-aware segmentation}

\citet{hu2019topology} introduced a differentiable topological loss based on persistent homology, and \citet{clough2020topological} extended it to cardiac imaging.
For tubular structures, clDice \citep{shit2021cldice} proposed a differentiable soft-skeleton metric.
The Skeleton Recall Loss \citep{kirchhoff2024skeleton} pre-computes ground-truth skeletons on CPU with over 90\% memory reduction.
Recent work has critically examined topology-aware losses \citep{arora2025srlcritique,joo2026cts}.

\subsection{Deformable and dynamic convolutions}

Deformable Convolutional Networks (DCN) \citep{dai2017deformable} introduced learnable offsets for adaptive sampling.
Dynamic Snake Convolution (DSConv) \citep{qi2023dynamic} constrains offsets along a one-dimensional path for elongated structures.
\citet{yu2024dscformer} proposed a dual-branch architecture combining DSConv with SegFormer for road crack segmentation.
Our work differs in addressing multi-class segmentation, introducing DTKD, and providing parameter-matched controls showing that DSConv's geometric bias provides no measurable benefit.

\subsection{Knowledge distillation for segmentation}

Knowledge distillation \citep{hinton2015distilling} transfers knowledge from teacher to student by matching softened distributions.
Multi-teacher distillation \citep{you2017learning} combines multiple specialised teachers.
\citet{zhang2022camkd} proposed confidence-aware multi-teacher KD at the sample level.
\citet{liang2025taskspecifickd} demonstrated task-specific KD from vision foundation models for medical segmentation.
Our strategy differs by combining a task-specific teacher with a foundation-model teacher whose complementary strengths are empirically validated through multi-seed matched controls.

\subsection{Foundation models for segmentation}

SAM \citep{kirillov2023sam} and DINOv2 \citep{oquab2024dinov2} offer powerful vision foundation models.
LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} enables parameter-efficient adaptation.
\citet{ye2024samdefect} applied SAM for structural damage detection.
In our experiments, using foundation models as \emph{teachers} in knowledge distillation outperforms direct LoRA fine-tuning, though the comparison is limited by the absence of systematic hyperparameter tuning for LoRA baselines.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}

Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the HeteroDistill framework. Our approach enhances a SegFormer-B2 backbone at two levels: (1)~\emph{feature extraction} via a parallel branch, and (2)~\emph{knowledge transfer} via dual-teacher distillation (DTKD).

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/fig_architecture.png}
    \caption{Overview of the HeteroDistill framework. (a)~A SegFormer-B2 encoder with a parallel DSConv branch produces 3-class segmentation logits; the branch additively enhances only the crack channel. (b)~Dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD): Teacher~1 (DSConv+SRL, strong on pixel accuracy) and Teacher~2 (SAM-LoRA+SRL, strong on spalling component recall) are combined by equal-weight logit averaging.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{SegFormer-B2 backbone}

We adopt SegFormer-B2 \citep{xie2021segformer} as our backbone, providing a hierarchical Mix Transformer (MiT) encoder with four stages at resolutions $H/4$, $H/8$, $H/16$, and $H/32$ with channel dimensions 64, 128, 320, and 512. The all-MLP decoder fuses multi-scale features via linear projection and upsampling. We initialise from weights pretrained on ADE20K at $512{\times}512$.

\subsection{Parallel crack-enhancement branch}
\label{sec:dsconv}

We add a lightweight parallel branch (1.32M parameters, +4.8\% over SegFormer-B2) that produces a single-channel crack enhancement signal:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{\text{crack}} = \mathbf{z}_{\text{crack}} + \uparrow\!\mathbf{E}_{\text{crack}}
    \label{eq:dsconv}
\end{equation}
The branch fuses encoder stages~2 and~3 (at $H/8$ and $H/16$) via $1{\times}1$ projections to a hidden dimension $d{=}64$, followed by two parallel processing paths (one per spatial axis) and a two-layer head (Conv2d $64{\to}32{\to}1$, zero-initialised so the branch starts as a no-op).

\paragraph{Branch type does not matter}
Parameter-matched controls (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}, Table~\ref{tab:branch_compare}) comparing DSConv, DCNv2 \citep{dai2017deformable}, and standard convolution---all with ${\sim}$1.32M parameters and 3 seeds each---show no measurable difference across any metric.
We retain DSConv in the default configuration but do not claim the branch type as a contribution.

\subsection{Dual-teacher knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:dtkd}

Different model architectures excel at different quality dimensions: the task-specific DSConv+SRL model (Teacher~1) achieves higher pixel-level accuracy (IoU), while SAM-LoRA (Teacher~2) achieves higher component recall (CompR$_{\text{fg}}$), driven by substantially stronger spalling component preservation (CompR$_{\text{sp}}$ 86.0\% vs.\ 73.4\%; Table~\ref{tab:foundation}).

\paragraph{Teacher models} We use two pretrained teachers, both trained with SRL \citep{kirchhoff2024skeleton}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Teacher~1} ($T_1$): DSConv+SRL (task-specific, strong on pixel accuracy)
    \item \textbf{Teacher~2} ($T_2$): SAM-LoRA+SRL (foundation model, strong on spalling component recall)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Teacher ensemble}
The two teacher predictions are combined by equal-weight logit averaging:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{z}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,(\mathbf{z}_{T_1} / \tau + \mathbf{z}_{T_2} / \tau), \qquad
    \tilde{\mathbf{p}} = \text{softmax}(\tilde{\mathbf{z}})
    \label{eq:dtkd_ensemble}
\end{equation}
This construction is parameter-free and treats both teachers symmetrically. A per-class probability-space variant yields near-identical results (Supplementary~S2).

\paragraph{Distillation loss} The student is trained with:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}} = \tau^2 \cdot \text{KL}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{p}} \,\|\, \text{softmax}(\mathbf{z}_s / \tau)\right)
    \label{eq:kd_loss}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Total loss} The training objective balances segmentation and distillation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha) \left[\lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Dice}}\right] + \alpha \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KD}}
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.5$, $\tau = 4$, and $\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = 0.5$.
SRL is used only during \emph{teacher} training; the student does not use SRL, as it conflicts with the distillation objective (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).
An agreement-aware variant that modulates KD loss by pixel-level teacher agreement is explored in Supplementary~S1.

\subsection{Dataset}

We evaluate on the \textbf{semantic segmentation subset} of DamSegment \citep{damsegment2026}, which contains 1{,}500 pixel-level annotated images with three classes (background, crack, spalling) at $512{\times}512$ resolution, organised into three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard; 500 each).
We split the data into train/val/test sets of 1{,}200/150/150 images, stratified by tier.
Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset} shows representative samples; crack pixels range from 0.3\% to 7.9\% and spalling from 1.0\% to 11.7\%.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

We report complementary metrics:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$}: Mean IoU of crack and spalling classes.
    \item \textbf{IoU$_{\text{cr}}$}, \textbf{IoU$_{\text{sp}}$}: Per-class IoU.
    \item \textbf{BF1}: Boundary F1 score (2-pixel tolerance) \citep{csurka2013good}.
    \item \textbf{clDice}: Centreline Dice via skeletonisation \citep{shit2021cldice}.
    \item \textbf{CompR}: Component Recall---fraction of ground-truth connected components for which $\geq$50\% of pixels are correctly predicted. Additional component-level metrics (CompP, CompF1, PathCont) are defined in Supplementary~S6.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Single- vs.\ dual-teacher KD}
Neither single teacher matches the dual-teacher result: T1-only achieves 69.0\% and T2-only 69.2\%, both below the no-KD baseline (70.4\%) and well below DTKD (72.3\%).
This indicates that a single teacher's soft labels can introduce more noise than useful information when the teacher lacks complementary knowledge.

\paragraph{(b)$\to$(c): Adding DTKD}
DTKD provides substantial improvement: mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ +1.9, IoU$_{\text{sp}}$ +2.8, clDice$_{\text{cr}}$ +1.5, and CompR$_{\text{fg}}$ +2.6.

\paragraph{Effect of SRL (rows e--f)}
\label{sec:srl}
SRL \citep{kirchhoff2024skeleton} penalises low predicted probability at ground-truth skeleton pixels:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{SRL}} = 1 - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} p_{\text{crack}}(s)
    \label{eq:srl}
\end{equation}
Adding SRL to student training under DTKD (row f) reduces performance compared to the SRL-free pipeline (row c): mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ 71.2\% vs.\ 72.3\% ($-$1.1), suggesting an optimisation conflict between SRL and KD objectives. Multi-seed experiments confirm that the SRL path is unstable (std 2.42 vs.\ 0.52 for DTKD, Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{DTKD: benefits and limitations}
DTKD adds +1.9 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ in a single run; multi-seed matched controls confirm +1.5 with 5--7$\times$ variance reduction (Table~\ref{tab:factorial}).
The $2{\times}2$ factorial reveals that DTKD benefits require the parallel branch (architectural affinity with Teacher~1).
The student surpasses the direct teacher ensemble by +1.5--1.6. Cross-seed stability improves modestly (std 0.70$\to$0.52).
DTKD's hyperparameters ($\tau$, $\alpha$) are robust: excluding $\tau{=}2$, all settings fall within 0.9\% of the default (Supplementary~S4).

\paragraph{SRL: useful for teachers, harmful for students}
SRL-trained teachers produce better students (+1.2 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$) despite SRL degrading the teacher's own component coverage. As a student training component, SRL interferes with soft-label supervision ($-$1.1 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$, single run). This asymmetry---that optimal teacher and student recipes differ---has broader implications for distillation pipeline design.

\paragraph{Indirect use of foundation models}
Under the evaluated LoRA configurations, directly fine-tuning SAM or DINOv2 underperforms task-specific architectures by 2--4\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$.
Using them as teachers yields better results, though the comparison is limited by the absence of hyperparameter tuning for LoRA baselines.

\paragraph{Branch geometry: a null result}
Parameter-matched controls (Table~\ref{tab:branch_compare}) show that DSConv's snake-shaped sampling provides no measurable advantage over DCNv2 or standard convolution across all segmentation and topology metrics.
The parallel branch provides useful capacity, but its geometric inductive bias does not matter.

\paragraph{Failure modes and limitations}
The model underperforms on (i)~thin hairline cracks ($<$2 pixels wide) and (ii)~co-occurring crack--spalling regions.
DamSegment comprises images from a single dam site with patch-level splitting; spatially adjacent patches may share visual characteristics.
A feature-cluster split sanity check (Supplementary~S5) shows that models do not collapse ($\pm$3 points), but DTKD's ranking advantage is not preserved---confounded by halved training data and different test composition.
Test-set metrics were observed during development; the multi-seed analysis (Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}) suggests DTKD's contribution is more modest than the single-seed +1.9: the multi-seed gap is 1.0 point (70.8\% vs.\ 71.8\%).
Cross-dataset transfer remains weak (mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ $\leq$ 18.7\%).
Additional analyses including loss exploration, ensemble analysis, engineering quantities, and deployment workflow are provided in the Supplementary Material.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented HeteroDistill, a segmentation framework for concrete dam defects that combines a parallel feature-extraction branch with dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD).
On DamSegment, HeteroDistill achieves 72.2--72.3\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ at 51 FPS with no additional inference cost from distillation.
DTKD is the primary contributor (+1.5 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ confirmed by multi-seed controls, 5--7$\times$ variance reduction), while a parameter-matched branch comparison shows no measurable benefit from geometric inductive bias.
The lightweight student model is positioned as the segmentation module for automated dam inspection workflows.
