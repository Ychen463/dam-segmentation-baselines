\opening{Dear Editor,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled \textbf{``HeteroDistill: Automated Crack and Spalling Segmentation for Concrete Dam Inspection via Heterogeneous Dual-Teacher Distillation''} for consideration for publication in \textit{Automation in Construction}.

\medskip
\textbf{Motivation and scope.}
Automated segmentation of cracks and spalling on concrete dam surfaces is critical for structural health monitoring, yet existing deep-learning methods optimise pixel-level metrics without preserving the structural continuity of detected defects. A fragmented crack prediction may achieve adequate IoU while misrepresenting the true crack path, leading engineers to underestimate damage severity. This paper directly addresses this gap, which we believe aligns well with the scope of \textit{Automation in Construction} in advancing intelligent inspection and monitoring of civil infrastructure.

\medskip
\textbf{Summary of contributions.}
We propose HeteroDistill, a segmentation framework that augments SegFormer-B2 with \textbf{heterogeneous dual-teacher knowledge distillation (DTKD)}, transferring complementary knowledge from a task-specific teacher and a foundation-model teacher (SAM-LoRA) via equal-weight logit averaging, with optional pixel-level confidence modulation. Multi-seed matched controls (5 conditions $\times$ 3 seeds) confirm that heterogeneous teachers produce a +1.5 mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$ gain with 5--7$\times$ variance reduction, while single-teacher distillation provides no reliable benefit---supporting teacher diversity as a key contributor.

We additionally report: (i)~a \textbf{negative result on geometric inductive bias}---parameter-matched controls show that Dynamic Snake Convolution provides no advantage over standard or deformable convolutions for crack topology, challenging the assumption that specialised convolution geometry is necessary; (ii)~Skeleton Recall Loss benefits teachers but harms students in distillation; (iii)~indirectly leveraging foundation models through distillation outperforms direct fine-tuning on small-scale industrial datasets. Zero-shot cross-dataset evaluation on S2DS confirms cross-domain robustness (18.7\% vs.\ 0.1\% mIoU$_{\text{fg}}$).

\medskip
\textbf{Significance.}
HeteroDistill achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DamSegment benchmark (72.2--72.3\% foreground mIoU, 83.0\% CompR$_{\text{fg}}$) with practical deployment advantages: no inference-time overhead from distillation, 5--7$\times$ reduced training variance, and improved cross-domain robustness. The parameter-matched negative result on convolution geometry provides guidance for practitioners selecting architectural components for elongated-structure segmentation. These results offer practical guidance for deploying deep learning in data-scarce dam inspection scenarios.
